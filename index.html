Government Bonds Get Dumped Worldwide as Iran Tensions Reignite

A global bond selloff pushed G7 yields to their highest level since 2000, compounded by the US striking Iran near the Strait of Hormuz for the first time in weeks — a one-two punch that spooked stocks and pushed Fed rate-hike odds toward 70%. Morning Brew

Global sale. Bloomberg calculates that G7 developed economies saw their average government bond yields — which move opposite bond prices — rise to the highest level since 2000. The UK’s 30-year bond yield hit its highest point since 1998, Japan’s 10-year yield broke 3% for the first time since 1996, and France’s 10-year yield neared 2008 highs — all as investors demand higher premiums for holding long-dated debt amid fears of unsustainable deficits and persistent inflation.

Stateside. Stocks slipped yesterday as US Treasury yields rose slightly, a month after hitting their highest level since 2007. The odds that the Fed raises rates at its next meeting have nearly doubled to almost 70% since last week.

Iran reignites. The US military struck Iran near the Strait of Hormuz yesterday in response to recent attacks on commercial ships, and President Trump warned Iran would be hit “again at a much harder and higher level” if it retaliates. Iran promised “crushing and devastating blows” in return, ending weeks of relative calm and pushing oil prices up almost 6% this week.

Why it matters. Rising yields mean higher borrowing costs for governments, consumers, and businesses everywhere — right as G20 finance ministers convene in Asheville, NC this week to discuss how the Treasury plans to handle the bond market.

Columnist | Daily Dad

They Do Complain a Lot. It’s Probably Fine. Daily Dad

They do complain a lot. It’s probably fine. Maybe they’re just trying to get out of school. Maybe they just want attention. Maybe it will work itself out. You know it’s probably nothing. You don’t want to make a pointless trip to the pharmacy or to the doctor, let alone the urgent care clinic.

But you know what? You’re probably not going to look back and regret taking your kids’ feelings and complaints seriously. If anything, you’re going to look back and feel terrible about the time you made them go to school with a broken wrist. You’re going to feel so guilty in the emergency room when the thing turned out to actually be something. You’re going to hate that you blew them off, that you didn’t believe them, that you minimized their pain.

Of course, every parent — especially when their kids are babies — can be a little extra. You don’t need to be a hypochondriac or a crazy person. But taking your kids seriously ages better than the alternative. And you can do this without overreacting, without being anxious or needlessly stressed out.

Treat what they’re telling you with good faith. Act like the symptoms matter, act like they mean something. Get it checked out. Hear them. Better to be glad it was nothing than feel awful when it turns out to be something.
