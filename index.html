Tesla Pulls the Plug on the Cybertruck

Elon Musk confirmed the polarizing pickup will stop production at the Texas Gigafactory by mid-2027, closing the book on a vehicle that hit just 10% of its sales goal and became a lightning rod amid Musk's political turn. Morning Brew

Tesla will stop producing the Cybertruck at its Texas Gigafactory by mid-2027, Musk said yesterday. He said the move is meant to make room for the company's autonomous Cybercab. The decision to nix what was once Tesla's most hyped vehicle comes after the Cybertruck became a commercial flop.

At the Cybertruck's 2019 unveiling — when Tesla's design chief broke its supposedly bulletproof window — Musk billed the futuristic pickup as Tesla's "best product ever." Tesla bet the Blade Runner-inspired stainless steel design, planned $40,000 price tag, and a 500-mile-range version would make it the first EV to dominate the pickup market. But after Covid delayed its initial release, the pickup's late-2023 arrival in showrooms was met with lukewarm demand, several recalls, a $60,000 price, and a range that never surpassed 325 miles.

Few takers: While Musk predicted yearly sales would reach 250,000 vehicles, just under 73,000 have been sold in total so far, per Motor Intelligence. Sales dropped sharply after Musk vocally backed President Trump, and the truck turned into a polarizing symbol amid his controversial effort to downsize the government — US sales fell to 20,237 in 2025 from 38,965 in 2024, and are down a further 28% this year.

Looking ahead: After the Cybertruck rides into the sunset, Tesla will have just two human-driven models in production, the Model 3 and Model Y (its airborne Roadster isn't scheduled to start production until 2028). The company is going full throttle on a nationwide rollout of its two-seater autonomous Cybercabs, already ferrying passengers in Austin.

Italian Chef Fired for Refusing to Put Pineapple on Pizza

An Italian chef working in Australia says he was wrongly fired for refusing to top pizza with pineapple, calling the dish an "insult to his culture." Morning Brew

Australia's Fair Work Commission dismissed Davide Tamburella's claim because he filed it too late, not because he was wrong. His former restaurant, La Costa in Queensland — home to a 52-foot-tall landmark called the Big Pineapple — has since closed. His bosses cited his language and communication style, including an incident where he broke his hand punching a metal railing; it was allegedly the third time in four years he'd refused the topping. One 2023 poll found 72% of Australians love pineapple pizza. Tamburella has since found a new job at a Brisbane Italian restaurant that keeps pineapple off the menu.
