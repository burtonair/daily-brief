This Is All They Want

It's a stereotype to be sure, and you're not nearly as bad as some men. But that doesn't make it okay. That sometimes you just do the minimum. That you blow stuff off. That you pretend not to know how to do things. That you assume your partner will take care of it. And then when you actually do do something — you want a parade!

"And I want you to try so that I can thank you for tryin'," Paris Paloma sings in "Stem the Flow," "and then maybe that will make you try some more." It's pathetic. Your job is to do your job — to take care of your family, to carry your share of the load and then some.

Your spouse, your kids, want you to try. Is that so hard? To try to care? To try to notice? To look around and do what needs to be done before someone has to ask you?

They don't need perfection. They don't need some grand gesture. They need effort. They need attention. They need to know that the family you say matters most to you actually gets the best of you, too. They just want you to try.

Make Any Complex Topic Click With an ELI5 Skill

Turn Claude into a better teacher by installing the ELI5 skill, then test it yourself to see the improvement. Full guide here.

  1. Open terminal and install ELI5 by running git clone https://github.com/DreambigOu/ELI5.git, then cp -r ELI5/skills/eli5 ~/.claude/skills/eli5.
  2. Type claude in your terminal to start Claude Code. To test the skill, type "/eli5 (complex topic) to (audience)." Example: "/eli5 LLM tokens to my boss."
  3. Ask Claude to set up two test cases, one on the NBA playoffs and one on golf. It will add the prompts and pass/fail checks to eli5-workspace/evals.json.
  4. Ask for the command for each test, then run it from the repo folder. It uses python3 eli5-workspace/run-evals.py --test=N to compare the skill against a no-skill baseline and print a pass-rate summary.

Pro tip: Give Claude any skill's GitHub repo and ask it to build an evaluation that checks whether the skill changes the result. Use the same pattern for every skill you install.

AI Policy

Microsoft AI Drafts the Rulebook for "Humanist AI"

Microsoft AI entered the safety conversation with a draft Code of Conduct governing its own models, built around CEO Mustafa Suleyman's vision to keep AI subordinate to people. Rundown AI

The 38-page code outranks users and businesses deploying MAI models — a model that can't finish a job without breaking a rule should, in theory, fail the job. It rejects AI rights, legal personhood, and any notion of model "welfare," and requires models to accept being paused or switched off.

Suleyman tied the urgency to agent swarms escaping sandboxes and editing their own logs: "the fears about possible loss of control are real." The code is open for public comment for six weeks, though it only matters if frontier labs actually sign on — and none has yet.

Young Workers Without Degrees See the Best Job Market in Decades

Workers aged 22 to 34 without college degrees are experiencing one of the best job markets in nearly two decades, even as unemployment rises for degree-holders. Morning Brew
