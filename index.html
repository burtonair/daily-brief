August Jobs Report Blindsides Wall Street, Raising the Odds of a Fed Rate Hike

The US added 162,000 jobs last month — more than triple what economists expected — but slowing wage growth and a shrinking tech workforce complicate the picture as the Fed weighs its next move on Sept. 16. Morning Brew

The numbers. The US added 162,000 jobs in August, according to government data released yesterday — more than triple the number economists polled by Dow Jones had predicted. Unemployment held steady at 4.1%.

Where the jobs came from — and didn’t. Restaurants and bars added 59,000 workers, construction and manufacturing grew by 22,000 and 16,000 respectively, and public schools gained 42,000 employees. But the information sector — which includes tech jobs — shed 23,000 workers, which some analysts said could reflect AI automating white-collar work.

The catch. Wage growth slowed to 3.1%, down from 3.7% a year ago, and inflation outpaced it. The trading odds of a rate hike jumped from 49% to 59% yesterday as Wall Street bet the healthier job market gives the Fed room to focus on inflation at its Sept. 16 meeting.

Why it matters. President Trump issued a blistering threat that if the Fed doesn’t cut rates instead, he’ll end trade with nations running a trade deficit with the US — a list that includes the EU and China — setting up a collision between the White House and an independent Fed.

You’re Going to Need Slack Daily Dad

“So it goes with parenting. If you’re behind, if you’re stretched too thin, if you haven’t prepared for a moment like this, you’re not going to be able to meet it.”

This week, Ryan Holiday revisited a conversation with bestselling author Daniel Pink about how ordinary time with your kids is the real quality time. It doesn’t mean you have to make a given day special — it means actually being here for it.

On the Daily Dad Podcast, Ryan made a related case for being more understanding — and less hypocritical — with our kids. We shouldn’t hold them to standards that even we, as adults, fail to meet in our own way.

Slack, presence, grace: different words for the same discipline. The moments that test us as parents rarely announce themselves in advance, and the only real preparation is having left ourselves room — in time, patience, and expectations — to meet them.

Pabst Blue Ribbon Turns a $70K Beer Heist Into a Marketing Campaign

After thieves stole roughly 34,000 cans of PBR and Old Milwaukee from a Southern California warehouse, the brewer is offering a $20,000 reward — and leaning hard into the publicity. Morning Brew

In two back-to-back swipes, thieves stole more than 1,500 cases — about 34,000 cans worth ~$70,000 — from a Southern California warehouse. One shipment vanished en route to Arizona; another was picked up by a group that forged documents to pose as a subcontractor, police said.

Pabst CEO Greig DeBow is now offering up to $20,000 for information, joking in a TikTok post that the thieves “look cool with a garage full of PBR.” The brand is following KitKat’s lead, which similarly capitalized on a 400,000-candy-bar theft earlier this year with a “Stolen KitKat Tracker” that won a Cannes Lions trophy.
