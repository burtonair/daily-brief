AI Is Upending the Hiring Process

Need a new job? Just use AI to polish up your résumé, submit an application to an AI-powered tracking system, interview with an AI agent, and then pass an AI background check. Morning Brew

Apply and demand. Career advisors and universities are increasingly encouraging job seekers to use AI to craft résumés and cover letters, but that leaves recruiters trying to figure out whether a candidate is a good communicator or just good at using AI. While 60% of hiring managers say they can identify AI-generated applications, only about 25% actually can, per a Software Finder survey. AI has also made it easier to apply for jobs: the average job opening drew 89 applications in Q1 2022, per hiring platform Greenhouse — three years later, each opening attracted 243. To keep up, 90% of US employers now use AI screening, per a Stanford study, though those tools also turn away talented prospects.

A hire calling. Making it through the application gauntlet just moves the AI encounter to the interview: nearly two-thirds of US job seekers say they've been interviewed by an AI bot, according to Greenhouse.

Passing inspection. Clear the interview and there's still one more AI hurdle: an exhaustive digital background check pulling information from dozens of sources — so any past run-in with the law, a questionable old post, or a college goatee phase has a good chance of surfacing.

Our Hiring Czar Thinks Cold LinkedIn DMing Works

Morning Brew Inc.'s VP of Talent Acquisition, Lily Mittman, on what actually happens on the receiving end of résumés, cover letters, and cold DMs. Morning Brew

On standing out: "Make sure you know the company you're interviewing with — things like the company's recent big wins, knowing some of the people on the team, knowing some names you can reference. Coming prepared with some good questions at the end is another way to show you did your homework."

On referrals: "Eight out of 10 times, we meet with somebody who's an internal referral. If an employee we know can vouch that someone is great and qualified, we assign a lot of value to that referral."

On cold outreach: "Find a connection to a mid-level employee — they probably aren't getting as many messages on LinkedIn as somebody who's super senior, so they may be more likely to take the time to connect."

Glossary

A Guide to the Work Culture Buzzwords of LinkedIn

This quarter's new hiring jargon, decoded. Morning Brew

  • Job-hugging: sticking to your current role — less a trend than a reaction to the job market's cooldown after the 2021–2022 "great resignation."
  • Lily-padding: job-hopping across roles or industries to gather broad skills before settling into a senior job — though it can set off alarm bells with some employers.
  • Career catfishing: applicants who exaggerate qualifications or accept a job and never show up — or employers dishonest about a role's responsibilities or culture.
  • Doomjobbing: absent-mindedly submitting job applications for dopamine hits rather than a real fit — often less effective than fewer, stronger applications.
