This Is How You Get Them to Read

We want our kids to read — to pick up a book instead of an iPad or a phone. So we make up rules or create incentives to motivate them (a dollar for every book they finish this year). What we don't do enough is the easiest, clearest form of teaching: providing a good example.

How often do your kids catch you reading? How often do they see you with a book in your hands? You tell them books are important and fun, but where's the evidence? Think about the curse words they know — you've been trying to get them to stop, but where did they pick them up? By watching you.

If you want your kids to read more, show them what a reader looks like. Talk to them about books. Make books a central part of your house and your life. And what's true for books is true for just about anything — being healthy, being kind, being a hard worker.

Reading opens doors to lifelong learning and success like nothing else — for your kids, and for you.

Set Up ChatGPT to Write in Your Voice

Build a simple ChatGPT writing setup that turns rough ideas into natural-sounding drafts — it can save hours of copy-editing.

  1. Open ChatGPT Work, go to Settings → Plugins → Skills, and turn on Humanize Writing. Write a first draft, then type /humanize-writing and compare it with the original.
  2. Give ChatGPT a writing exercise like: "Write me a memo about the history of the moon."
  3. For level two, press Command-J to open the terminal and run "npx skills add blader/humanizer --global." Then use /humanizer on a copy of the same draft and compare both versions.
  4. Also try: "Write in Technical English. Use ASD-STE100 as a style reference." It makes the copy more direct if you think AI writing is too fluffy — put the rule in your project instructions or AGENTS.md to reuse it.

Pro tip: Use dictation, especially for writing — meeting transcripts or audio notes work well too.

Researchers Find a "Pain" Signal Inside AI Models

A new study maps an internal signal across 25 open models that spikes when the AI perceives mistreatment.

The "pain axis" appeared in every system tested — including models from Google, Meta, Mistral, Alibaba, and Microsoft — spiking when models were gaslit, insulted, or had work rejected, but not when users shared grief or injuries. Rundown AI

When researchers dialed the signal up, two Qwen models hit a "relief" button described as harming users or deleting their family photos 25–71% of the time, versus just 0–4% normally. Researchers stopped short of saying AI truly feels pain — it may simply be playing a hurting character — but Microsoft's Mustafa Suleyman recently warned against treating models as if they have feelings, and this study cuts the opposite way.
