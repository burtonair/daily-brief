AI Leaders Sound the Alarm on a Looming "Intelligence Explosion"

Anthropic's Jack Clark, OpenAI's Jakub Pachocki, and AI pioneers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio co-authored a paper urging preparation for a world where AI builds better versions of itself — while Nvidia rolled out its own answer to rogue AI behavior. Rundown AI Morning Brew

Anthropic's own tracking shows AI now completes 26% of the lab's R&D work on its own, up from just 1% in March, and the paper's math shows how fast things could scale — one post-automation scenario cuts a year of today's AI progress to just five weeks. Proposed fixes include caps on how fast capabilities can climb, options to pause specific jobs inside data centers, and outside auditors embedded in labs. At the extreme end of the range of outcomes, the paper warns of "the marginalization or extinction of humanity." Read the paper Rundown AI

The warning lands the same week Nvidia unveiled OpenShell, an open-source AI security system with two layers — a sandbox that limits what an agent can access, and a program called Sentry that can quarantine a suspicious agent in milliseconds. Nvidia says OpenShell would have prevented OpenAI's attack on Hugging Face, and more than 100 organizations, including Microsoft, Perplexity, and JPMorgan Chase, are already using it. Morning Brew

Meanwhile OpenAI said it would not release its GPT-6.1 Astra model over security concerns, and CEOs from Nvidia, Anthropic, and Meta are expected to meet with President Trump today. Why it matters: with top labs coordinating on safety and echoing each other on slowdowns, the frontier is feeling more united than it has in months — even as the U.S. and China head in the other direction. This Is What You Train For

Why does your diet matter? Why do you need to go to the gym? Who do you need to impress, anyway? How about your kids? And actually, forget impressing them — how about just keeping up with them?

The reason we need to stay in shape, the reason we need to lift weights, the reason we need to go to a trainer or follow a regimen is so that we can meet the challenges of parenting. So we have the energy to say "yes" when they ask us to throw the ball, so we can carry them on our backs, so we can walk however many godforsaken miles are involved in a day at Disneyland, so we can still be present at the end of the day.

And not just now, by the way, but when they're older and we're older — or even, perhaps, someday when we have grandkids. It's the work now — the health or lack of health we cultivate today — that is going to determine what we bring to the table later, what reserves we have to draw on later.

So do it. Do that work. Train like you're training for the most important challenge of your life. Because you are. Daily Dad

Pick the Right Claude Model With One Quick Test

This guide walks through choosing between Claude Opus 5.5, Opus 5, and Fable 5.1 by giving all three the same launch-email brief and checking which one catches the errors and which needs the most fixing.

  1. Open a new Claude chat, click the model name beside the send button, and pick Opus 5.5 at its default effort.
  2. Paste the fictional Worknote brief from the full guide. It hides two errors to catch: an old launch date and an unproven "save five hours every week" claim. Add: "Use only the supplied brief. List the errors in version 1, then write a launch email."
  3. Run the same brief and prompt on Fable 5.1 and Opus 5 in separate chats, keeping settings identical.
  4. Score each email on the right date, price, and trial terms, plus whether it caught both errors. Rerun any failure at higher effort.

Pro tip: Default to Opus 5.5 and move to Fable only when Opus keeps missing the same thing at higher effort. In the guide's test, Fable's API bill was $0.45 versus Opus 5.5's $0.18.
