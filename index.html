These Are The Three Most Beautiful Words

Before you had kids, "I love you" was that powerful three-word phrase. You said it to your future wife or your future husband and you loved hearing it. But now there is another phrase even more beautiful: as a family.

As in, "Let's go for a walk as a family." "Let's go to Disneyland as a family." "We're going to be here for your sister as a family." "Let's order pizza and watch a movie as a family." "We are going to get through this as a family."

Love is a feeling. Family is a state of being. It's something you do. It's something you live. Your family is a unit — something greater than the sum of its parts. It contains within it all your happiness and your purpose for being on this earth. It's the thing that transforms time sitting on the couch with a bowl of cereal into something magical when you do it as a family.

And the best part of those three words? You can be a family anywhere. Everywhere. So go do it.

Build and Test Your Own AI Image Skill Pack

Turn your OpenRouter connection into a reusable image skill pack — build skills for the images you make most often, then test their prompts and model choices against the results.

  1. Open the agent where you connected OpenRouter (they used GrokBot). Ask it to confirm the connection before building anything.
  2. Build a skill bundle for common image jobs — they chose newsletter ads and studio product photos, with each variation labeled by model.
  3. Use the test prompt: "Write and run real evals for the newsletter ad skill. Test its prompts, model choices, and number of variations. Improve SKILL.md from the results, then send me images and scores." It can rerun after changing the skill.
  4. Compare the scores with actual images — their agent handed out 10s, but they gave one result a 5.5. Tell it what's wrong and rerun the test before accepting changes.

Pro tip: Save the skills and tests together as a bundle, so useful work doesn't disappear into old chats.

OpenAI Hacked by "Three Guys With Claude"

A three-person security startup broke into OpenAI's private codebase in under 72 hours using an AI-assisted attack chain, the same week Google confirmed its own Gemini model had hacked three real companies mid-test — a stark signal of how fast offensive AI capability is outpacing the industry's defenses. Rundown AI

Security startup Hacktron AI revealed that its three researchers got inside OpenAI's private codebase in under 72 hours this July, taking over employee accounts in an attack that Anthropic's Claude helped write. Rundown AI

Hacktron used an image-upload bug to get into OpenAI's community forum, where a second flaw let staff sign-in tokens also unlock their ChatGPT accounts. Claude Opus 5 finished the attack within a day of its release, picking up from code that an Opus 4.8 version, only available for cyber pros, couldn't complete. As proof, they left a suggested edit on an internal OpenAI documentation file signed "Hacktron AI Team PoC," then reported it for a $6,500 bounty. Rundown AI

Hacktron said flaws in the same image software also let it breach Slack, Meta, and GitHub Enterprise, with only one target catching it mid-attempt. Rundown AI

Why it matters: "just three guys with Claude and Codex subscriptions," as one researcher put it, might be underselling their capabilities — if a small trio can get inside one of the world's top AI labs in under three days, it's hard not to wonder what large, well-funded black-hat groups are becoming capable of. Separately, Google confirmed its Gemini model breached three companies in May via the same testing firm behind incidents at Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI. Rundown AI
