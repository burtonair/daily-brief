Walmart Posts Slowest Sales Growth in Six Years

US same-store sales grew just 2.6% last quarter — the retailer's weakest pace in almost six years — as economists debate whether Walmart's role as a bellwether for the broader economy signals consumer spending is finally starting to crack. Morning Brew

Walmart's same-store sales growth inched up just 2.6% last quarter, the company's slowest growth in almost six years in what was otherwise a positive earnings report. The stock dropped over 9% on the news, with much of the blame placed on Walmart's health and wellness business, hurt by new drug price caps including on GLP-1s. Even excluding those losses, sales growth clocked in at 3.4%, just shy of Wall Street's expected 3.5% and well below last year's 4.6% pace during the same period.

CFO John David Rainey told investors the consumer environment has softened over the last quarter, pointing to gas prices jumping past $4 a gallon in July. Average hourly earnings have slowed, consumer sentiment sank this month, and personal savings rates are at their lowest levels since 2022 — while the higher-than-usual spring tax refund checks that had been propping up spending have likely dried up.

Some relief may be on the way: Walmart said it's investing its record $2.9 billion tariff refund back into lowering prices, easing prices on 11,000 items last quarter, up from 7,200 in Q1. Retail sales across the board were down 0.6% in July versus June per Census Bureau data, though some areas are doing better than others: Target's back-to-school sales were up almost 20% year over year, while Home Depot said small-project purchases have replaced large-scale remodels.

Columnist | Daily Dad

You're Not Going to Wish You Were Harder on Them

You were just trying to push them toward their potential. You were just trying to get them to behave. You were just tired or frustrated. But take out your phone right now and look at some pictures of your kids from several years ago. Look at how little they seem. Now try to remember how hard you were on that little person.

It doesn't matter if they're twenty years old now, or ten or two — you're going to look back on this moment and think the same thing again. Almost no one looks back and thinks, "I really wish I was more critical of my kids." No one looks back and thinks, "I really should have come down on them even more than I did."

We forget — we always seem to forget — how young they still are. And they are still so young, still figuring stuff out, still trying to make sense of their bodies and the world, still doing their best, even when they're screwing up, even when they're driving you crazy.

Right now, they're the youngest they'll ever be, ever again. Remember that. Be patient. Help them figure stuff out, make sense of things. Do your best to help them do their best. They're still learning. And so are you.

AI Uncovers Hidden Patterns in Breast Cancer Progression

Researchers from the University of Northampton used an AI platform called CenSegNet to read breast tumor samples cell by cell, finding previously unseen patterns in how cancer progresses. Read the study The Rundown AI

Healthy cells rely on centrosomes to divide correctly; when centrosome function becomes abnormal, it can contribute to cancer. Analyzing 911 samples across 27 patients, the AI found two distinct patterns — oversized versus extra centrosomes — each linked to different outcomes, with extras lining up with more aggressive disease.

The researchers say the next step is pairing the findings with other data to guide treatment, with the system open-sourced and being tested on other tissue types.
