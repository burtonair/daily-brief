Surprise Jobs Slowdown Sends Stocks to Record Highs

The US added just 29,000 jobs in September, far below the 84,000 economists expected — but Wall Street read the weak report as a sign the Fed will hold off on another rate hike, pushing the Nasdaq to a new intraday record. Morning Brew

Healthcare, usually the most reliable job-creating sector, added just 17,000 positions versus its 33,000 average over the past year. Construction and manufacturing each added a modest 9,000–11,000 jobs, while AI-exposed industries like business services and information shed workers. Government statisticians also revised down July and August job counts by a combined 60,000, suggesting this summer's hiring strength was partly a mirage. Read more Morning Brew

Unemployment ticked up from 4.1% to 4.2%, though that's largely because more people are entering the labor pool rather than jobs disappearing. The bigger worry: yearly wage growth slowed to 3%, the lowest since the pandemic, meaning pay still isn't keeping pace with inflation. Morning Brew

Why it matters: This is the last monthly jobs report before the midterms, and Wall Street now puts the odds of an October rate hike at just 20%, down from 24% the day before. Stocks surged on the news, with Nvidia leading the Nasdaq to a record and Tesla jumping after Q3 deliveries beat expectations. Morning Brew
