Food • Health

Despite Health Risks, Raw Milk Is Having a Moment

Farmers are reporting growing raw milk sales, with gallons sometimes going for $20+, as MAHA-aligned health influencers push the unpasteurized drink—even as the CDC ties it to thousands of illnesses and dozens of hospitalizations. Morning Brew

Many Americans are rejecting modernity and embracing tradition not by limiting screen time but by drinking their milk raw. Unlike the stuff sold in most grocery stores, raw milk isn't heated to kill harmful pathogens in a process known as pasteurization. Lawmakers in dozens of states are pushing to loosen restrictions on raw milk sales, and a bipartisan House bill would allow it to be sold across state lines, which federal law currently prohibits.

Why drink it raw? The craze is largely fueled by health influencers and anti-pasteurization advocates aligned with the Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) movement, who insist raw milk is healthier—a claim public health experts say isn't supported by evidence. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. took a shot of raw milk at the White House, and Norwegian soccer star Erling Haaland says he drinks it all the time for his stomach, skin, and bones.

The risks are real: Between 1998 and 2019, bacterial outbreaks tied to raw milk sickened 2,600 Americans and were linked to 225 hospitalizations, per the CDC, and raw milk products are 45 times more likely than pasteurized ones to cause hospitalization. The FDA linked nine recent E. coli cases this year to raw milk and cheddar cheese from one prominent advocate's own farm.

¿Soy Milk? Dairy Industry Fights to Keep the Word ‘Milk’ for Cows Only

Dairy producers argue plant-based drinks shouldn't get to call themselves milk, and courts are taking sides: the UK's Supreme Court ruled Oatly can't use "milk" on its oat products, and a Swiss court blocked Alpro's "Not M*lk" branding. Meanwhile dairy-free milk sales have dropped over 5% three years running. Morning Brew

Buyers Still Want Fancy Dairy Despite Inflation

Americans ate a record 6.8 pounds of butter each in 2024, with shoppers splurging on $60-per-pound farm butter and $15 pints of ice cream. Parisian retailer La Grande Épicerie sold 19 tons of butter last year, up 300% from 2023. Morning Brew

Culture • History

How ‘Got Milk?’ Became a Famed Ad Campaign

Early 1990s ads for the California Milk Processor Board satirized empty-carton disasters rather than featuring milk itself. At its peak, an estimated 80% of US consumers saw the slogan on any given day; it retired in 2014. Morning Brew

Culture • Beauty

Why Some People Prefer to Use Milk Externally

The dairy product best enjoyed with cookies has become a popular ingredient in beauty products—a way to enjoy milk even if you're lactose intolerant.

Credit goes back as far as Cleopatra's milk baths in ancient Egypt, which may have inspired recent products from the likes of Hailey Bieber. Milk can be found in creams, moisturizers, soaps, and serums for your skin, and is also a way to keep hair hydrated.

Which milks show up in these products? Cow, goat, buffalo, and—this is not a misprint—donkey. There are also nondairy options: "Some formulas may contain milk proteins or acids," cosmetic chemist Ron Robinson told New Beauty. "Some of these products don't contain any cow's milk at all. But instead, they may contain vegan alternatives such as nut milks." Morning Brew
