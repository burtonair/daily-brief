Columnist | Daily Dad

This Is the Most Important Decision You Make

When you're in a bad mood, your kids think it has something to do with them. Even if you try to explain that it's really the stress on your shoulders, or a coworker's stupidity, or your boss's short temper, those are complex emotional issues that are hard for a young kid to understand, abstracted as they are from a kid's own lived experience.

In his memoir, Bruce Springsteen writes about how his father's moods affected him as a child: "As a boy I just figured it was the way men were, distant, uncommunicative, busy within the currents of the grown-up world... If you aren't spoken to, you're not worth the time. If you're not greeted with love and affection, you haven't earned it. If you're ignored, you don't exist... I sensed what was being said: I was an intruder, a stranger, a competitor in our home and a fearful disappointment. My heart broke and I crumpled."

We can grant that parents often have it hard — that their own parents were flawed, that their childhoods weren't what they'd have chosen. But they, like all people, got to choose how they acted around their kids. Too often, people allow the toxic patterns of their past to overwhelm their capacity to choose correctly with their children in the present.

Our moods and choices are always affecting our kids, changing how they see the world and themselves — making them feel worthwhile or worthless, safe or vulnerable, and creating a pattern that becomes hard not to follow. Which is why we have to remember what Voltaire said: "The most important decision you make is to be in a good mood." Daily Dad

Get Started with Jev, TypeSafe's New Decision Model

Jev takes a message and a question like "Is this urgent?" and returns an answer software can act on — it's built for sorting jobs, not writing emails. At $0.042 per million input tokens (nothing for output), testing it is essentially free.

  1. Think of one sorting job you repeat: tagging feedback, labeling article topics, or spotting urgent requests.
  2. Open the TypeSafe Playground and paste in a made-up message to test, like a customer locked out of a workshop login.
  3. Ask one multiple-choice question — e.g. "What is this message about?" with options like access, billing, sales, other.
  4. Try to trip it up: a mislabeled sign-in issue still correctly routed to "access," while a vague "Can you help?" went to "other" for a human to check.
