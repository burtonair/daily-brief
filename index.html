Canada Strikes Back With 50% Tariffs on US Goods

After putting away the carrot, Canada reached for the stick: Ottawa announced tariffs of up to 50% on US exports, mirroring the US duties that took effect after weekend trade talks collapsed. Morning Brew

Canada will tax $20 billion worth of made-in-the-USA goods at 15%, 25%, and 50%, matching US duties dollar for dollar. Taking effect after Labor Day, the tariffs hit 700 American products, from motorcycles and chainsaws to cheese and frozen octopus.

Canada says the goal is protecting local businesses undermined by US tariffs and pressuring American politicians ahead of the midterms — seafood duties, for instance, are thought to target Maine, where Sen. Susan Collins is campaigning in a close race. But commentators note the tariffs on food and clothes will hurt Canadian shoppers too, and economists expect Canada to suffer more than the US given how much more of its trade depends on its southern neighbor.

The rhetoric kept escalating: Trump floated renaming Lake Ontario "Lake America," Ontario Premier Doug Ford discussed taxing Canadian power exports to the US, and Trump has threatened doubling car and truck tariffs to 50%. Still, top officials on both sides say they're potentially willing to return to the negotiating table.
