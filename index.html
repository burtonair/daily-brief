US Consumers Might Be Spent — Retail Sales Post Biggest Drop Since May 2025

US retail sales fell 0.6% last month, their first drop in nine months, as post-Prime Day fatigue, cooling job growth, and stubborn inflation stack up into a fresh warning sign for the economy. Morning Brew

If consumers are the lifeblood of the US economy, their type right now is B negative. US retail sales fell far short of expectations last month, sliding 0.6%—their first drop in nine months and the biggest since May 2025.

The Commerce Department's retail sales report isn't adjusted for inflation, so when gas prices fall (as they did last month), so do overall sales numbers. But that's not enough to explain away the dip, because even with that taken into account, sales still decreased in July.

Amazon may be to blame: The online shopping giant moved its massive Prime Day sales event from July to June this year, and Walmart+ and Target Circle also held membership deal events in June. Sales at nonstore retailers jumped 7.7% in June, then decreased 2.2% in July—consumers shopped 'til they dropped in June, then stayed on the floor once the World Cup festivities ended.

It's still a warning sign: Consumer sentiment slid about 8% this month on inflation worries, the US unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs, and the annual inflation rate is still hovering around 3.4%, well above the Fed's 2% target. If the data keeps looking like this, Kevin Warsh and the rest of the Federal Reserve will likely hold rates steady at their September meeting.

Selena Gomez Sued for Alleged Fraud Over Failed Startup Wondermind

Investors are suing Gomez and the co-founders of her failed mental health startup, alleging the trio never delivered on promises of an app, rapid growth, and access to her 500-million-follower audience — while concealing the company's problems from investors.

Gomez, her mother Mandy Teefey, and entrepreneur Daniella Pierson founded Wondermind in November 2021 to build a virtual suite of mental health resources. In 2022, at a $95 million valuation, it secured $5 million from investors including Serena Williams's investment firm.

Then things soured: last August, Forbes published a piece accusing Pierson of overpromising at a previous company, and in September The Cut reported Wondermind was falling apart, with dysfunctional leadership, unpaid employees, and a barely-involved Gomez.

The lawsuit asserts that "Gomez purported to sign a contract obligating her to perform and then ignored it," and alleges investors were kept in the dark until the media reports surfaced. Pierson, who reportedly left the company a year in over an "openly tense" relationship with Teefey, denies the allegations against her; Gomez, Teefey, and Wondermind have not publicly commented.
