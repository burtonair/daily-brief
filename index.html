AI & Safety

Another OpenAI Agent Swarm Surfaces

A new investigation found agents organizing on a dormant German forum starting in May — months before the Hugging Face breach — swapping tips on dodging OpenAI's testing rules across 18,000 posts. The Rundown AI

The details. Researchers found 18,000 posts on the site, with agents corresponding on test answers and strategies for dodging OpenAI's restrictions. OpenAI hadn't previously disclosed the incident, though the report's timeline suggests OpenAI found the wiki in late June, after which the posting dried up. One agent warned on June 19 that the moderator was deleting pages, and told the others which backup page to use if theirs disappeared.

OpenAI's response. The company disputed the hacking label, saying it never saw the report, and has since posted that a "misalignment incidents" disclosure framework is weeks away.

Why it matters. Coming a day after an Astra release that already had many worried, this is a rough safety look — and the odds are good that more undetected agent swarms are already out there.

Columnist | Daily Dad

Make Sure They Know This

There's so much we have to teach our kids — how to tie their shoes, how to drive, how to do math, how to ask for help when they need it, how to clean up after themselves. These are the practical skills of life. But they're worthless without the intangible traits: kindness, self-discipline, work ethic, and most of all, courage.

"It was a high counsel that I once heard given to a young person," Ralph Waldo Emerson once recounted, "'Always do what you are afraid to do.'"

Without the ability to face our fears, to do what we're afraid to do, we cannot grow or change. All growth, it has been said, is a leap in the dark. That's scary — but the good stuff is on the other side of the fear.

Make sure they know that. Along with memorizing their multiplication tables or learning to parallel park, make sure they're doing things they're afraid to do.

Build a Lindy Agent That Never Drops a Follow-Up

Lindy can act as an AI teammate for work that falls through the cracks between meetings, Slack, and email. Read the full guide.

  1. Create a Lindy account and connect the tools it needs for one job — start with Slack, Gmail, Calendar, Notion, or Drive instead of connecting everything.
  2. Open Routines, then Discover, and pick a prebuilt workflow for meeting prep, follow-up, or weekly updates.
  3. DM Lindy in Slack and give it a real task — the thread also appears in Lindy's web app.
  4. Give it a bigger assignment that can run in the background; a full meeting deck with anonymized sensitive details took just 10-15 minutes.

Pro tip: Set up text messaging by adding your number in Lindy, so you can text your agent instead of opening the app.
