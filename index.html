Inflation Sizzles — and a Rate Hike Looks Inevitable

Kevin Warsh looks days away from raising interest rates for the first time as Fed chair after August's CPI report came in hotter than expected, complicating Trump's push for cuts ahead of the midterms. Morning Brew

The data. The consumer price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4% in August and 3.4% over the past 12 months, undercutting the narrative from July's report that inflation was cooling. Core CPI jumped a higher-than-expected 0.3% for the month, and traders are now pricing in a 90% chance of a quarter-point hike next week, per CME Group data.

What's driving it. Gasoline prices climbed 3.9% over the previous month and 27.4% over the past year, fueled largely by the war in Iran. Computer software and accessory prices skyrocketed 25.4% year-over-year — the largest annual increase on record — even as smartphone prices fell 12.2%.

The political bind. The stage is set for the typically tight-lipped Warsh to raise rates for the first time in three years, despite being selected by Trump with an expectation he would cut. High prices are also weighing on the upcoming midterms. Not everyone agrees a hike is wise: one economist told CNN a rate increase paired with already-high energy prices could slow the economy more than intended. Treasury yields remained near multiyear highs yesterday.
