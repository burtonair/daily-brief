Economy

Labor Day Gas Prices Have Never Been This High

Pump prices for this time of year hit their highest level on record over the holiday weekend, driven by continued oil disruptions from the Iran war and the Russia–Ukraine war. Morning Brew

The details. Gas cost an average of $4.15 per gallon yesterday, according to AAA — gas never surpassed $4/gallon on Labor Day before this year. The previous holiday record was $3.82/gallon, set in 2012 (the all-time record is $5.02/gallon from June 2022). The new record is ~30% higher than gas prices were this time last year.

Why now. Gas prices typically move seasonally — rising through spring and summer, then descending by Labor Day. This year is an exception: Iran has constrained tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz since its war started in February, while Ukrainian drone attacks have disabled some Russian refineries. US gas inventories were 6% below average for the last full week of August, per the Energy Information Administration.

You're feeling it even without a car. Diesel — fuel for the freight trucks that carry everything from food to clothing — spiked almost 60% from last Labor Day to hit a record $5.85/gallon. Prices for domestic flights to 10 popular US destinations were up ~20% compared with last year.

Looking ahead. The EPA moved up the date refineries can switch to cheaper winter gas blends, from Sept. 15 to Sept. 1, which may offer some relief. But the outlook stays uncertain: Iran plans to expand its Strait lockdown in response to new US strikes, and US refineries are already working near-total capacity.

Be Here for This Moment

It felt like there was so much summer left. Like there were more beach days ahead of us. Vacations and weekend camping trips still to take. More visits to the splash pad, to the pool, to the grandparent's house because, well, you still do have to work sometimes. Picnics and concerts and barbecues with family and friends to look forward to.

But here we are, in early September. Labor Day has come and gone. School is in session. There are pumpkins outside of grocery stores and your kid is already talking about their Halloween costume. There are leaves on the ground. There's the faintest chill in the air. How did the last month, the summer, go by so fast? August has, as Taylor Swift said, "slipped away into a moment in time."

The summer is gone. September is here, and with it, a new season, a new school year. A new chapter in your kid's life and yours.

So be present for it. Be in the moment with the people you love. Be there, doing exactly what you're supposed to be doing — not scrolling on your phone, not thinking about what's next, not planning your week, not stressing about work. Be with your kid, with your family, and make the most of the moment while you have it. Before it all slips away into a moment in time.

AI & Medicine

Insilico's AI-Designed Drug Hints at Slowing Aging

Insilico Medicine published trial data showing rentosertib, a drug its AI designed for a lung disease, left patients reading as biologically younger on all six "aging clocks." The Rundown AI

Rentosertib treats idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, where scarring slowly chokes the lungs; Insilico's AI picked the target protein and drew the molecule. Last year's 42-patient trial produced blood samples later used for the aging study, compared against a broader UK Biobank database.

Six separate aging-clock AI models rated treated patients as younger, with one group's estimates falling 2.7–3.5 years. Notably, the dosing with the clearest aging signal differed from the one with the biggest lung-function gains — suggesting effects beyond disease relief.

Why it matters: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has said a real medical breakthrough could shift AI sentiment more than any marketing campaign — and this is an early look at what a race of AI-designed drugs reaching the market could look like.

AI & Society

U.S. Voters' Rare Bipartisan Issue Is Fearing AI

A new NBC News poll of 7,105 adults found 70% feel more worried than excited about AI, with concern spanning both parties even as usage keeps growing. The Rundown AI

52% use AI very often or sometimes, up six points from June 2025, but just 18% trust AI-generated information most or almost all of the time. 69% oppose having a data center nearby (45% strongly), against just 31% who support one.

70% believe AI is costing people jobs, and majorities expect it to hurt schools and elections. 81% say Washington's AI rules fall short, and 44% trust neither party to handle AI policy — a live issue heading into the midterms.
