The Most Profitable US Airline Can’t Shake Oil Shocks

Delta cut its full-year outlook and missed Q3 expectations as surging fuel prices strain an otherwise booming business — a result that could be an omen for the rest of the airline industry. Morning Brew

Yesterday, Delta cut its full-year earnings outlook from $6.50–$7.50 per share to $5.10–$5.60, and trimmed its free cash flow forecast from $3 billion–$4 billion to $2.5 billion. Adjusted revenue landed at $17.58 billion, up 15.7% year-over-year but below analysts' estimates of $17.76 billion, per Bloomberg; profits of $1.13 billion also missed expectations. Morning Brew

“All of it's fuel,” CFO Erik Snell said when asked why Delta cut its guidance. The carrier paid $500 million more in fuel costs in Q3 than it projected in July, bringing its quarterly fuel bill to $4.1 billion — a 62% increase from a year ago, with the war in Iran throttling the global oil market a key driver. Morning Brew

Travel demand remains strong and is expected to continue through the holidays, with industrywide ticket prices up 23.4% year-over-year as of August. Airlines are responding to the fuel squeeze by adding lucrative long-haul routes, cutting cheaper ones, and expanding premium seating — a strategy that helped Delta offset costs, with premium-seating revenue up 18% in Q3. Investors will get a clearer industry-wide picture when United, Southwest, American, Alaska, and JetBlue report earnings later this month. Morning Brew

One edge Delta has that its competitors don't: it's the only major US carrier that owns its own oil refinery. Morning Brew

Walmart's Robots Still Can't Lift the Big Bags of Dog Food

A new Wall Street Journal report shows Walmart has spent billions over the last decade trying to fully automate its 200 US supply-chain buildings — with uneven results so far. Morning Brew

  • Walmart is racing to catch up with Amazon, which dethroned it as the world's biggest company by 2025 sales.
  • In Walmart's automated grocery facilities, electric bills can balloon to over $800,000 per month, compared to $250,000 for a manual warehouse.
  • About 5% of warehouse robots are being fixed at any given time — down from 10% last year — according to robotics partner Symbotic.
  • The company missed its January automation targets, but by August, over 65% of stores were getting some automated freight and more than half of e-commerce goods were being moved by automated systems.
