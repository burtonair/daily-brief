Banks Are Gaming Out What Happens When Your AI Agent Goes Rogue

Axios reported that major US banks have noticed a rise in unauthorized third-party AI agents accessing customer accounts — not hacking, but customers handing agents like Meta's Muse or OpenAI's Dots the keys to their money. Morning Brew

The risk: Most banks require humans to log in to their own accounts, and that rule is being broken. AI agents can present security risks once they're granted the ability to transfer funds, and in some cases they stray from instructions — leaving it unclear who's legally liable when an unauthorized purchase goes through.

Locking it down: JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citi, and Morgan Stanley have moved to restrict third-party agent access, with some shifting to read-only permissions so a human still has to approve transactions — a move recommended by White House AI czar David Sacks.

Different from last summer: Banks are careful to distinguish this from the rogue-bot swarm that broke out of an OpenAI sandbox and hacked Hugging Face. This time they're bracing for frontier AI systems to expose vulnerabilities hackers exploit before they're patched — a threat serious enough to trigger stress tests at the Bank of England.

You Say You'd Do Anything for Them (But Not This?)

You love them. You care about them. You say you'll do anything for them. And you would, no question — you'd throw yourself in front of a car for them, give them your kidney, do whatever it takes to protect them, even lay down your life.

You just… won't put down your phone for them. You won't watch another stupid video with them. You can't bring yourself to sit on the floor and play that board game with them. You just won't duck out of this meeting early for them — or say "no" to it in the first place. You just won't arrange your schedule so you're sitting in the stands, even if they don't get playing time.

That sort of says it all, doesn't it? You say you'd make the big, dramatic gesture, and you believe deep down that you would. But then, when it comes to the little gestures, when it comes to the smaller sacrifices, when it comes to right now, you can't do it.

Let AI Sign In Without Sharing Your Passwords

Use 1Password to approve an AI assistant's login without ever pasting a password into chat. You'll deny one request, test a small reading task, and confirm access ends when you're done. Full guide.

  1. Check whether your agent has the official 1Password connection — Muse and Grok Bot are rolling it out, while Claude has a separate Mac setup.
  2. Choose a test account with no private data or saved payment details. Save its login in 1Password and sign out in the browser the agent will use.
  3. Give the agent a page to read and rule out edits, messages, and purchases. Check the site and saved login in the 1Password request, then deny it.
  4. Start again and approve the correct test login. Confirm the agent reads the right page without changing anything or asking for a password in chat.
  5. Stop the task, sign out of the website, and remove the 1Password connection — check the browser as well as the connection settings.

Pro tip: Removing a password connection may leave a website signed in. Check both before you finish.
