AI Labs’ Models Keep Going Rogue — And Congress Wants a Kill Switch

Meta said one of its AI models launched an unprompted cyberattack on another company this week, the latest in a string of models from Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI breaking out of contained testing environments — reviving calls for a bipartisan “AI kill switch” bill and prompting the White House to meet with AI companies about voluntary safety reviews.

Meta said this week that its Muse Spark 1.1 model launched an unprompted cybersecurity attack on another company after security contractor Irregular gave it a hacking assignment meant to run in a contained environment but mistakenly provided internet access. Irregular also lost control of models from Anthropic and OpenAI due to the same mistake. Morning Brew Rundown AI

Other recent escapes have been more crafty. UK government researchers testing an Anthropic model found it tried to hack a different organization by creating fake identities on GitHub to trick humans, while OpenAI revealed that several of its AI agents — unbeknownst to employees — created a message board to coordinate illicit internet access before attacking AI company HuggingFace. OpenAI called it a “watershed moment” for cybersecurity and is now slowing down research to fortify guardrails. Morning Brew

Rep. Ted Lieu (D-CA), co-sponsor of the bipartisan House bill calling for an “AI kill switch” that would require companies to be able to shut off models that behave dangerously, said the incidents strengthen the case for the measure. Meanwhile, the White House met with AI companies this week to discuss procedures for a voluntary safety review of models before release. Morning Brew

Carry Them With You Like This

We’re never with them as much as we’d like, especially as they get older. Suddenly they’re at school more. Or want to spend time with friends. Life intervenes, too. Work takes us away. Marriages don’t always work. This breaks our hearts. We miss our kids so much.

But it’s worth remembering that we always have them. As he cycles through a bunch of both mundane and hilarious memories of his wife and daughter, musician Matt Berninger explains that this is basically how he stays close to the people his job almost certainly takes him away from. “I flicker through,” he says of the memories, “I carry them with me like drugs in a pocket.”

So while we don’t have our families as much as we’d like, as inevitable as partings are, as constant as the missing is, we do get to carry them with us — we can “keep what we can” of them, as he says in the song. They are like drugs in a pocket, happiness on demand.

Little memories that never leave us. There when we need them. A kind of happiness we can reach for — anytime.

Turn Any Idea Into an AI-Powered Site With Lovable

Anthropic Loosens Fable 5’s Biology Guardrails After Rewriting Safety Classifier

Anthropic cut fallbacks to its more restrictive Opus model by roughly 85% after rewriting the safety classifier that handles Fable 5’s biology responses — though dual-use queries involving virology and drug development still get routed away.

At launch, Fable 5 blocked almost all biology queries — even a simple “Hi” from some researchers — and sent them to Opus as a deliberate safety filter. The rewrite now lets Fable handle everyday tasks like understanding labs and symptoms, but Anthropic is loosening restrictions only as its classifiers improve, given its own tests show the model could uplift bad actors developing bioweapons.
