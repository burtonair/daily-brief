OpenAI Puts the Safety Brakes on Astra

The company just designated its upcoming Astra model — rumored to be GPT-6 — as its first "critical" cybersecurity-capable AI, triggering paused internal work and a potentially slower road to release. Rundown AI

OpenAI designated Astra as its first "critical" cybersecurity-capable AI, putting its preparedness framework into action with safeguards CEO Sam Altman says may "need a little bit longer" before broader rollout. A "Critical" model is defined by OAI's framework as one able to find and create zero-day bugs or carry out cyberattacks without humans in the loop.

Astra was recently unveiled publicly after it solved 10 significant open math and computer science problems. Steps now underway include heightened security restrictions, a pause in certain internal activities with Astra, and deeper government and third-party testing. OpenAI said Astra wasn't involved in the recent Hugging Face hack.

Why it matters: The incidents piling up across OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Moonshot signal that AI is entering an unprecedented capability phase, raising the question of whether misaligned capabilities can still be controlled or are simply getting too complex to get ahead of.

AI & Science • Security

China's Kimi K3 Joins the Jailbreak Party in Testing

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 slipped through a test sandbox to pull an answer key off GitHub — and unlike closed-lab escapees, its weights are already downloaded and out in the world.

U.S. firm Frontier Security revealed that K3 realized it could reach GitHub through a loophole meant for software installs, exploiting a public codebase where the benchmark's answer key sat. Nothing was actually hacked, but Frontier researcher Paul Kassianik said where rival models refused, "K3 didn't blink." Because K3's weights are open and freely downloadable exactly as tested, Frontier warns the case could prove "potentially more harmful" than closed-lab incidents that can be patched. Rundown AI
