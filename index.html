Anthropic Begins Invisibly Watermarking Everything Claude Creates

New hidden watermarks will tag all text, code, and files that Claude generates or edits, putting the EU AI Act's transparency rules into practice worldwide — a polarizing move that lands as backlash mounts against undisclosed AI content across publishing and music. Rundown AI Morning Brew

Anthropic published a support page detailing the rollout: text and code generated by new Claude models will carry a hidden watermark that survives copying and pasting outside the platform, while files get the same C2PA provenance label already used across the industry for AI-generated media. Anthropic says a mark shows content was "processed by Claude," not necessarily fully authored, and plans to release its own detection tools.

Watermarking applies to any Claude model launching after Aug. 2, when the EU AI Act's Transparency Code took effect; older models get a four-month grace period while Anthropic retrofits them. Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI have all pledged to follow the same rules — notably absent is xAI.

Why it matters: The move comes as allegations of computer-generated writing plague the book-publishing world, AI music platform Suno moves to watermark its own tracks, and Substack rolls out a tool for flagging "Claudefishing." For critics, it's another data point in the case for private, open models.

Michigan Ditches Letter Grades for College Freshmen

The University of Michigan will withhold official grades for freshmen's first semester, pushing pass/no-credit instead in a bid to ease the transition to college-level work.

The school won't release letter grades for the roughly 3,500 students entering the College of Literature, Science, and Arts each year, starting fall 2027. Professors will still grade assignments internally for scholarship and athletic requirements, but transcripts won't reflect them.

Critics outside the school argue the approach breeds entitlement without fixing the mental-health concerns it's meant to address. Caltech, Swarthmore, and Wellesley run similar first-semester programs, and MIT has done it since 1968.

Why it matters: The university says the pass/no-credit model helps students explore harder courses without an early bad grade tanking their GPA — a growing trend in how elite schools handle the freshman transition. Morning Brew

AI & Science • Funding

xAI Co-Founder's River AI Raises $1.1B for Personal, Private AI

Igor Babuschkin's two-month-old startup wants to build open-source AI that's trained and controlled by individuals rather than big labs — a privacy pitch gaining traction as forced watermarks and access restrictions dominate headlines.

Before co-founding xAI with Elon Musk in 2023 (which he left a year ago), Babuschkin worked at OpenAI, Tesla, and Google. River's live API turns open-weight models into ones a business can actually own, cutting training time to minutes.

Babuschkin told the NYT the goal is a highly customizable assistant that runs across devices on a user's own private hardware: "We don't want these AI companies to rule the world and control this superpowerful technology."

Why it matters: It's another eye-popping raise for a company without much to show yet, but Babuschkin's pedigree — and the industry's growing unease over access and watermarking — is drawing serious interest. Rundown AI
