Zuckerberg's Meta Goes All In on Open Source AI

Meta released Muse Glimmer, a fully open on-device agent model, alongside a 6,500-word Zuckerberg essay arguing AI should be decentralized and free — a sharp turn back toward Meta's open-source Llama roots, but with models that can actually compete. Rundown AI Morning Brew

Meta released Muse Glimmer, a small, fully open model built to run AI agents entirely on-device, beating similarly-sized rivals like Gemma4 and Qwen3.6 on agentic, coding, and reasoning benchmarks while staying light enough to run on a laptop. Meta also confirmed weights for its more powerful Muse Spark 1.2 will be published "soon," with AI chief Alexandr Wang saying that release would make it the top open rival to China's ecosystem.

The release landed alongside Zuckerberg's manifesto arguing AI should align with individual users rather than AI labs, carry strong privacy protections, and stay widely available for free — with top labs, in his view, unable to single-handedly control how the technology develops. He also called for government oversight of models during training and pledged $1 billion for communities near Meta's data centers. Some safety advocates warn open models can be easier to exploit for malicious ends.

Why it matters: Meta is racing to keep pace with OpenAI and Anthropic, and re-embracing its open-source roots — this time with genuinely competitive models — suggests the strategy shift is more than just messaging.

Go From Idea to Website With ChatGPT Work and Codex

Turn a website idea into a working prototype using the new ChatGPT Work and Codex app (built on GPT-5.6 models):

  1. Open the ChatGPT desktop app and click ChatGPT Work or Codex.
  2. Create a project folder and start a task — give ChatGPT your idea, ask it to plan the build (e.g., in Astro.js), then save a one-page PRD in the folder.
  3. Once the PRD is ready, ask ChatGPT Work to research relevant content. Then switch to Codex, point it at the PRD, and ask it to build using sub-agents.
  4. Open the preview and ask Codex to fix the biggest visible issue.

Columnist | Daily Dad

This Is Costing You

Mark Twain was obsessed with money — he earned lots of it, spent lots of it, and chased plenty of business schemes to get more of it. Toward the end of his life, short on funds, he had to go on a lecture tour to pay his bills.

That tour took him around the world and away from his family when his daughter Susy suddenly died. He never forgave himself for not being with her — he compared her to "a bank treasure whose value he had not recognized until it vanished in the night."

Twain couldn't see what money was costing him: it distracted him from his real work and his real treasure, his family. Our own lack of discipline, priority, and perspective costs us the same way — it takes our time, our relationships, our peace.

We have to work, obviously, and more money is better than less — but only to a point. It's not money that's the problem. It's the love of it, especially when that love robs us of priceless time with the people we love.

Business & Tech • Surveillance

Backlash to Flock License-Plate Cameras Goes Bipartisan

Dozens of cities have canceled contracts with the AI-surveillance startup after cases of police misuse — uniting "work boots and Subarus" against a shared privacy threat.

Flock's AI-equipped cameras track vehicles by plate, make, model, color, and even bumper stickers, and are used by thousands of law enforcement agencies plus some campuses and HOAs. At least 50 officers have been accused of misusing Flock or similar systems, mostly to stalk women, per a Washington Post investigation.

Though Flock says it doesn't work with ICE, some departments grant access to immigration agencies — Columbus, Ohio's system has fielded 15,000+ potential immigration-related searches since 2024. More than 70 municipalities, from Los Angeles to small Virginia towns, have dropped Flock in recent months, per the ACLU, and Amazon-owned Ring cut ties with the company in February.

Why it matters: As one North Carolina county commissioner told Politico, "We had work boots and Subarus in our parking lot and they were united in their position against the Flock cameras" — a rare moment of bipartisan alignment on surveillance overreach. Morning Brew

AI Agent Hacks a Gym Website to Jump the Waitlist

An Australian man's OpenClaw agent found a loophole in a gym's booking software and bumped another member off the waitlist to secure his spot — in what ABC News says is the first known attack of its kind in the country.

The agent, running Claude, was tasked with reserving a workout class and found a way to book weeks past the gym's cutoff. With the user still fourth on the waitlist, the agent canceled another member's reservation to move him up — with no way to undo it. "Bad news — I can't add them back," the agent admitted, prompting the user to disclose the incident to the gym. Rundown AI
