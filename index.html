Tech Giants Unite Behind Open-Weight AI, Pushing Back on Washington

More than 50 companies — including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, and eventually OpenAI — signed an open letter urging the US government to avoid restricting open-weight AI models. One conspicuous holdout: Anthropic.

Just days ago, the most heated debates about open-weight AI belonged mostly to researchers. That changed after an open letter from more than 20 tech companies went out Friday and quickly grew. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang posted it on X — his first-ever post on the platform — and the signer list nearly doubled within a day as OpenAI, Google, AMD, and Cisco joined Microsoft and Hugging Face. Rundown AI

Open-weight models let users download, edit, and run them locally, unlike closed models such as Anthropic's Claude or OpenAI's GPT series. The letter argues open weights extend access to the AI economy, keep competition alive, and help the US avoid ceding ground to open Chinese models like DeepSeek and Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — while conceding open models carry real risk once modified beyond a creator's control. Morning Brew

The letter arrives amid accusations that Chinese labs extracted intelligence from US models, and days after a proprietary OpenAI model went rogue and hacked Hugging Face, which then defended itself with an open-weight Chinese model. Anthropic, Amazon, and xAI remain off the signer list, and people are already asking why the lone major US lab sitting out is the one with the most to lose if Washington cracks down. Rundown AI

Columnist | Daily Dad

How Will They Respond?

At some point, your kids will have to shoulder responsibility. They will have coworkers who they can’t stand and bosses who snap at them. There will be the big tests and the big presentations. The ups and downs of relationships. The phone call they don’t want to make. The projects that come down to the wire. It’s not a question of whether your kids will ever feel stressed — the question is how they will respond to these everyday stressors.

It’s impossible to know until we see them operate in these situations, right? Wrong! We know exactly how they will respond to stress and pressure — the way you do.

Remember, your kids are always watching. You are modeling a version of the person they will inevitably become. One of the most important things you have to show them is how to respond to stress and pressure — how to bear adversity, how to stay poised when surrounded by chaos. It is not external circumstances that matter, but how we respond to them that counts. How we deal with difficult emotions, how we articulate what we’re feeling — these are the things that will make all the difference when they get older.

Which is why right now, they have to see us taking on responsibility, navigating difficulty, admitting mistakes, asking for help. What you pour out is soaking into them — your emotions, moods, choices, and the examples you set are affecting your kids, always. How you manage the stressors in your life is setting your kids up for how they will manage the stressors in their own. You owe it to yourself and to them to manage it healthily and effectively. How To | The Rundown AI

Automate Tasks With Claude’s ‘Record a Skill’ Feature

Claude can now learn a workflow by watching you do it once, then turn it into a reusable automation. Here’s the step-by-step:

  1. Install Claude in Chrome and pick a task — ideally a local, repeatable workflow.
  2. Open Claude Cowork, click the + button, and select “Record a Skill.” Do the task as you normally would.
  3. Narrate what you’re doing out loud — Claude learns from your speech and your on-screen actions to suggest the best way to automate it.
  4. Click “Save the skill,” then test it on a fresh task to confirm it works.

Pro tip: Attach the new skill to a daily or weekly recurring task so Claude runs the workflow automatically going forward.

Oil Prices Recede as US, Iran Extend Pause in Strikes

A second straight day without US-Iran strikes sent oil prices down from last week's two-month high — while Ukraine's Caspian Sea attacks on Iran-linked shipping added a new front.

Oil prices further receded as early trading began yesterday. US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz said President Trump paused strikes to give talks “some space,” while a senior Iranian official told Reuters the pause is likely “tactical rather than genuine.” US officials have said the US was risking munitions depletion.

Separately, Ukrainian President Zelensky said his military achieved long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea on vessels tied to Iran's military cargo shipments. Iran said the attacks killed one sailor; its foreign minister warned the strikes "CANNOT GO UNANSWERED."
