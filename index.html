AI Research

MIT Shows GPT-6 Astra Can Rewrite Other Models' Weights Directly

New research suggests AI that improves other AI — no retraining required — may already be sitting inside publicly available models. The Rundown AI

Weights are normally set through training, since even small models have far too many to adjust by hand. MIT researchers found that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra changed just 0.016% of Alibaba's small Qwen model's weights to cut its habit of repeating itself — raising its math score from 7.9% to 48.1%. The same approach did little for Google's smaller Gemma 3, which lacked a single flaw like Qwen's repetition.

In smaller tests, Astra also named the game an AI had learned purely from its weights, and wrote a simple Breakout player from scratch without training. The authors say this could one day let AI upgrade itself or check other models for hidden problems.

Why it matters: Once models are strong enough to alter and improve their own weights, we're in major "takeoff" territory — where AI starts speeding up its own progress.
