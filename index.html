This Is What You Learn

There were the before times, and the after times. Before you had kids. After you had kids. Before, you thought you knew things. After, you know that everything is different. You know that you knew so little.

Life is like that—especially life with kids. You learn you had no idea what tired was. You had no idea how crazy things could be. You had no idea what happiness was. You had no idea what you were capable of. You had no idea what was actually important. You had no idea how little control you have.

Ten years ago, Ryan Holiday started writing The Daily Stoic — ten years into his study of Stoicism, and not yet a parent. Within months that would change, and a few years later, it would change again. Parenting has reshaped his understanding of Stoicism in ways he didn't expect, and tested his practice of it even more.

Because that's one thing the Stoics knew—as parents and as philosophers—you can't stop learning. Life is always teaching you, if you're willing to listen. Daily Dad

How (and Why) to Set Up Meta's Muse Desktop

This guide walks through setting up Muse on your desktop and using it for more than the phone app can handle.

  1. Download and install Muse for Mac, then sign in and go through the setup wizard, reviewing each local-app permission. Set most to read-only for now.
  2. In Settings, open Connectors and add some apps you use.
  3. Check the access requested by each connector and start with lower levels until you trust Muse — for example, set Gmail access to read-only.
  4. Give Muse a task, like: "I want to sell my computer monitor. Check local marketplace listings every day for similar HP monitors and log them to a spreadsheet so I can learn to build the best listing."

Pro tip: Pair Muse with the iPhone app and try asking it to do a task that uses your Mac. Read the full guide
