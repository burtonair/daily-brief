Not Everyone Can Afford This (But You Can)

For some people, it really is inconceivable. For some people, subsistence really is on the line. For some people, they really just can’t. They can’t do school drop-off or pickup. They can’t make it to every game. They can’t be home in the evenings. They can’t take an afternoon off to go do some fun family activity, let alone a week or two for vacation. They can’t even take time off when their kids are sick or struggling.

But you? You can. You could make it to Back-to-School Night. You’re just choosing something else over it. You could choose to be around more. You just won’t. You could choose to be more active, more aware, more involved…

It wouldn’t be free to do this, of course, but great things, wonderful things, and the important things in life rarely are. Having kids is a sacrifice. So is choosing to really be a parent.

Not everyone can afford to do it at the level that you can — not everyone is as fortunate as you are. But then again, being able to afford something and then willingly paying the price for it are not the same thing.

Turn a Product Photo Into Three Matching Ads

Using Nano Banana 2.1, you can turn one product photo into a matching set of campaign images — changing layout, testing a headline, and keeping the product consistent throughout.

  1. Open Google Flow, choose Nano Banana 2.1 in the standard image controls, and add a clear product photo. Check the usage cost before generating.
  2. Make one square image with a simple background and room for text. Compare it against your original photo before approving.
  3. Change one small detail, like a cloth’s color, and confirm the product and rest of the image stayed close to the approved version.
  4. Use the original and approved image as references to generate a portrait and a wide version, keeping the same product, light, and style.
  5. Add a short headline, check every word, then review the product details, crop, and text in the downloaded files before using them.

The NBA Wants to Put Microchips in Every Basketball

Commissioner Adam Silver says gram-sized sensors could go into basketballs as soon as next year, after tests in developmental, summer, and preseason games. Morning Brew

Players reportedly couldn’t tell the difference. The “smart ball” senses movement and trajectory changes, helping refs determine who touched it last, and could become a consumer product letting players dissect their shooting mechanics.

The league previously used Oura rings to track player health in its pandemic bubble, and wrist wearables for officials more recently. Players have voiced concern about how performance data like this might be used in contract bargaining. Some soccer leagues are experimenting with chips in balls too, and the NFL has used RFID ball tags for years.

AI & Security

Microsoft’s Nadella: Treat AI Like It’s Already Been Hacked

In an essay on X, Satya Nadella urged companies to guard advanced AI models like a powerful employee who could slip up or get hacked — complete with an “emergency brake” a human can pull mid-task. Rundown AI

Nadella argued engineers could once trace a misbehaving program back to its code, but today’s models can’t always be explained that way. He wants permissions and activity logs kept where the AI can’t edit them, no model grading its own work, and reasoning shown in plain words — “Neuralese,” or AI thinking in unreadable code, “isn’t an excuse to hide it.”

His closing line: the most trustworthy system is the one “that enables us to trust the model the least.” The essay reads as a direct response to reports that OpenAI’s newest techniques can hide part of a model’s reasoning from human monitors.
