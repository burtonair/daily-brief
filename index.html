US Unveils "Operation Economic Outcast" to Isolate Iran

The Trump administration rolled out the sanctions plan it had teased as "economic D-Day" — though Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent characterized it as more of a "warning shot" than a full-scale assault. Morning Brew

Bessent said the US will impose devastating sanctions against countries that enable Iran to illicitly trade in oil, crypto, aviation, gold, technology, and shipping, aiming for the "economic asphyxiation" of the regime. But he said Washington is giving countries time to "remedy their bad behavior" rather than risk "blowing up the global financial system" with immediate action. The US did announce immediate sanctions against 60 entities, individuals, and vessels accused of enabling Iranian "recklessness."

Bessent noted the UAE, Iran's biggest import partner, already cut trade ties with Iran last week, and said Trump is pushing other countries to follow. Asked about enforcement against China — which bought 80% of Iran's oil before the war — Bessent said no one would be exempt, though analysts doubt the administration will risk antagonizing Beijing with Xi Jinping's US visit next month approaching. Iran's oil exports to China have reportedly already fallen since the US began blockading the Strait of Hormuz.

The bet is that economic pressure forces Iran to lift its chokehold on the Strait, which has pushed up energy prices ahead of the midterms. But markets shrugged at the announcement, and some experts doubt the US has much economic leverage left after years of sanctions. Iran has vowed to retaliate, possibly militarily, even as Oman continues negotiations and Pakistan's army chief pushes renewed peace talks.

Be Proud of This

Of course her father would have preferred to be powerful. He would have preferred to hold some prestigious position, to be able to do what he wanted with his life. He didn't want to spend it digging sewage canals or waiting on entitled aristocrats, at the bottom of Rome's social hierarchy. Nobody would choose that life — but in a fable about the Stoic philosopher Epictetus, this father doesn't get a choice about his status, not in a place like ancient Rome.

He couldn't choose his fate, so he chose instead to focus on how he bore it. "We didn't choose this," he tells his daughter, "but we can choose not to add to our troubles by complaining about them." When she turns up her nose at their dirty job, he tells her, "How you do anything is how you do everything. And if you do it well, it can be beautiful, no matter what it is." He refuses to be humiliated, refuses to be broken. "No one has the power to degrade us," he says, "only themselves by being unfair and unkind."

Because whoever you are, whatever you do, you can be someone your kids respect, someone they can be proud of. How? By doing that work well. By taking it seriously. By taking yourself seriously. By taking your role as their parent, as their model, as their protector seriously. The single parent working long shifts in a restaurant is far more impressive than the half-assing stockbroker anyway, even if the market compensates these two roles very differently. The person who is committed, who is in command of themselves, who focuses on what's in their control — that is the true philosopher, not the one with the fancy job title.

You are taking care of your family. You are doing what needs to be done. That's enough to earn you the respect of yourself, and the people who matter. You are being what your kids deserve. Be proud, and wear it proudly. Daily Dad

Build a Reusable AI Design System with Open Design

A free AI design tool that extracts your brand's design rules, letting you generate consistent design assets on demand.

  1. Download Open Design and connect an existing AI agent such as Codex CLI.
  2. Click "Create New Design System," paste in your website, click Add, then Continue. Add a logo, images, or a DESIGN.md if it missed anything.
  3. Select the new design system and give it a concrete brief like "Build a 10-second animation explaining our product."
  4. For v2, give feedback like you would to ChatGPT — you can also annotate directly on the design or circle things you want changed.

Pro tip: Shape the idea and prompt in ChatGPT first, then build it out in Open Design's visual, Figma-like workspace.

AI • Enterprise

Thomson Reuters Builds Its Own Legal AI Model

The Westlaw parent retooled Alibaba's open-source Qwen, training it on decades of proprietary legal content. The Rundown AI

The two-year effort cost $40M in staff and compute, with the latest training run alone costing $450K. Thomson says it benchmarked ahead of Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and GPT-5.5 in certain areas, though testing so far is internal only. CTO Joel Hron compared renting AI to renting a house — you end up with no "equity that compounds" over time. An open-weights version will be released for researchers.

$40M sounds like a lot, but for a company that size it's closer to a year of API bills than a moonshot. Expect more content-rich firms to run the same math as training costs keep falling.

AI • Security

Chinese Hackers Double Attacks with DeepSeek

China's state-tied hacking crews are pulling off over twice as many attacks since integrating open-source models like DeepSeek, per Taiwanese research firm TeamT5. The Rundown AI

"DeepSeek is the AI of choice for Chinese hackers because it's relatively powerful with very low cyber guardrails," TeamT5 chief analyst Charles Li told Bloomberg. The tracked groups used it to write break-in code, raid a Taiwanese firm's email, and map a target via 1,000 addresses — researchers haven't yet caught pricier, better-guardrailed models like Kimi K3 in an attack.

The UK's AI Security Institute warned in May that AI's cyber capabilities are doubling every few months. The concerning part isn't the frontier models getting headlines — it's the cheap, downloadable ones with fewer guardrails.
