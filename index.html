Ternus Inherits Prosperity, Uncertainty as Apple CEO

John Ternus takes the big desk from Tim Cook today, ending Cook’s 15-year run atop Apple — and stepping into the job under pressure to catch Apple up in the AI race just as costs and competition for talent are climbing. Morning Brew

The handoff. New Apple CEO John Ternus takes over from Tim Cook today under more pressure than his predecessor faced, leading the company into the AI future. Ternus, 51, joined Apple in 2001 and made his name overseeing the company’s hardware division.

What he’s inheriting. Apple is playing catch-up after Apple Intelligence and Vision Pro landed with thuds in 2024, with a new AI-powered Siri meant to compete with ChatGPT and Claude arriving in two weeks. Meanwhile, hundreds of former Apple employees have fled to OpenAI, and AI companies are gobbling up chip supply, driving costs Apple is now passing on to customers.

Cook’s legacy. When Cook replaced Steve Jobs in 2011, he inherited perhaps the biggest shoes in tech — and delivered: shares climbed 2,000% under his watch, Apple briefly hit a $5 trillion market cap in July, and the company will sell 255 million iPhones this year, up from 72 million in 2011. The 65-year-old isn’t fully stepping away — he stays on as executive chairman, including as a liaison to President Trump on tariffs.

Hard launch. Cook will attend Apple’s annual product event next Wednesday but won’t take the stage. Instead, Ternus will face the public for the first time as CEO to introduce a new foldable iPhone.

What To Do When It’s Not What You Imagined Daily Dad

Things got screwed up. Somebody dropped the ball. There was a miscommunication or a delay. You’re not going to get what you were promised, what you wanted, or what you’d prefer. You were caught off-guard. What do you do? You get a little frustrated. You get a little upset. It happens to the best of us.

And yet, when our kids do it, we come down on them. We tell them this isn’t age-appropriate, that they’re embarrassing us, that we refuse to reward this behavior. It’s true we shouldn’t reward it — but we should do a better job understanding it. Our kids have naïver hopes and expectations than we do, and even less experience managing disappointment. We shouldn’t hold them to standards that we, as adults, fail to meet ourselves.

We all get upset. We’ve all thrown little tantrums. We should try to get better at not doing this — but we should also be kinder to our kids when they’re doing something pretty natural, something they’ve seen us do plenty of times.

Connect ChatGPT to iMessage The Rundown AI

Turn ChatGPT Work into an assistant that reads and triages your iMessages, discovering the capabilities, limitations, and a few quick ways to make it work better.

  1. Open the ChatGPT desktop app on your Mac. Click Plugins > search “Messages” > install the plugin, then allow Automation, Contacts, and Full Disk Access.
  2. Start a ChatGPT Work chat and prompt: “Check my unread iMessages and let me know what I need to catch up on. Then text myself the recap.” Make sure you’re saved as your own contact.
  3. ChatGPT will ask for read access to each unread conversation — approve “for this query” or globally for the session.
  4. You’ll receive a text from ChatGPT summarizing your inbox.

Pro tip: The iMessage plugin is desktop-only, but you can click “Remote” in the iOS app to pair with your desktop or set a scheduled text-triage task. Watch the full video guide.

MapQuest Becomes Belle of the App Store After Defying Trump

MapQuest shot from obsolescence to the top of the App Store charts after refusing to rename Lake Ontario to “Lake America” on its maps — while Google made the switch for US users. Morning Brew

Amid renewed trade tensions between the US and Canada, Trump issued an order directing federal agencies to rename the border-straddling lake “Lake America.” “We’re not changing it,” MapQuest announced on X, and the post got 100,000+ likes after the account had failed to crack two likes on any other post last month.

  • MapQuest for iOS became the No. 1 Navigation app in the US, and reached No. 8 overall in the US and No. 2 in Canada.

On the other hand, some X users called Google “pathetic” after it renamed the lake for US-based Google Maps users, saying it made the change in keeping with the official federal database of geographic names — the same reasoning Google and Apple gave for the Gulf of America switch last year. As of last night, Apple Maps still showed “Lake Ontario.”

AI & Tech • Research

Anthropic Tests Automated AI Safety Research

Teams of Claude agents ran safety research on their own, training away 10 kinds of AI misbehavior with results coming in over 4x better on average than human experts handed the same job. The Rundown AI

Anthropic published new research where Claude agents cycled through literature searches, training, and scoring on 10 common failure types, including sycophancy, deception, and jailbreaks.

  • Fixes ranged from a 26% decrease in sycophancy to a 96% improvement for reward hacking, without lowering overall abilities.
  • Six veteran safety researchers saw just a 20% fix on deception under the same conditions; Claude averaged 85% across over 150 attempts.
  • A weaker Claude Sonnet 5 also spent 60 hours safety-training a pre-release Opus 4.8 build, using 15,000x less data than Anthropic’s own process.

Why it matters: Most AI roadmaps assume the tech will eventually take over AI research, and this is one of the first concrete signs of the handoff starting — even as fallout from OpenAI’s breach is still being uncovered, raising the stakes of trusting non-human systems with the safety work.
