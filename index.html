Business • Family Finance

Half of Parents Are Now Bankrolling Their Adult Kids

Financial support for grown children is becoming the norm, not the exception — driven by a tougher job market, soaring rents, and college debt. Morning Brew

Half of parents with adult children helped at least one of them financially in 2025, up from 45% in 2023, according to a Savings.com survey — and it's often more than an occasional gift card. Less than half of Americans age 18 to 34 say they're entirely financially independent from their parents, per a 2024 Pew report, and more than a third of those age 30 to 34 still aren't. The average yearly support now runs about $7,000 per family, according to AARP.

The shift is generational: today's young women are more likely to be self-sufficient than in the past, but young men have grown more reliant on their parents — 36% of 25-year-old men depended on parental cash flow in 2021, versus 23% in 1980. Researchers point to a tougher job market and rising costs, including rents and tuition-driven student debt, as the drivers.

It's not just money changing hands — nearly half of Americans age 18 to 29 now live with their parents, per the Federal Reserve, up from 37% in 2019. Most pitch in on groceries and rent, and many parents can afford the arrangement: the median baby boomer household is sitting on $432k in wealth, about 30% more than their own parents had.

Paid Family Leave Is Quietly Disappearing

The share of US companies offering paid family leave fell two points to 31% in 2025, as Deloitte and Zoom both scaled back the perk this year — Deloitte cut parental leave from 16 weeks to 8 for some workers, Zoom from up to 22 weeks to as few as 10. Morning Brew

Business • Auto

Minivans Are Suddenly Cool Again

Minivan sales jumped 21% last year versus just 2% for the overall auto market, per Edmunds data, as millennial parents — and even Uber/Lyft drivers — embrace the practicality and sliding doors. Morning Brew

Culture • Family

The Rise of Platonic Co-Parenting

A growing number of millennials are raising kids with friends instead of romantic partners — from "mommunes" splitting rent and childcare to apps like Modamily, which grew from 30,000 users in 2020 to 100,000 last year. Morning Brew

Culture • Parenting

Inside the Elite British School for Nannies

Norland College trains nannies for the ultrawealthy — including the actual Royal Family — and is about to become the world's first university dedicated to early childhood.

Just 300 students attend the four-year program in Bath, England, founded in 1892 as the oldest early-education college in the world; men weren't admitted until 2017, and non-EU students until 2022. Graduates learn everything from first aid to cybersecurity to martial arts to protect kids from potential kidnappers, and most field around seven job offers and six-figure starting salaries. The school will be renamed Norland University of Early Childhood on September 7. Morning Brew

Culture • TV

Why Grown Adults Can't Stop Watching Bluey

The Australian cartoon about a family of dogs racked up 45 billion streaming minutes last year — and a huge chunk of its audience is adults.

One popular Bluey-themed YouTube channel says half its audience is 18 to 35, and brands increasingly sell Bluey merch designed for adults. Unlike shows that "scream and strobe" kids into oblivion, Bluey is story-driven and tackles adult themes like infertility and the "trifficulties" of parenting — part of why it resonates with parents and non-parents alike, ahead of a soon-to-be movie.
