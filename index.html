The Rise of Ironman: How Pain Became a Growth Industry

Ironman racing — a 2.4-mile swim, 112-mile bike ride, and 26.2-mile run — is surging in popularity, with the sport's youngest competitors leading the charge. Morning Brew

Participation in Ironman and its shorter sibling, Ironman 70.3, surged globally in 2025: 250,000 registrants, with newbies up 10% year over year. Under-30 participation grew 35%, and the number of first-timers under 30 has more than doubled since 2019. In North America, the under-30 demographic jumped 23% for the full Ironman and 29% for the shorter race. Four new races were added this year, in Ruidoso, NM; Omaha, NE; Dallas, TX; and Jacksonville, FL.

The appeal isn't just the suffering — it's the money. There's a $6 million annual prize pool for professionals, and host cities see real economic upside: Ironman Wales in Tenby has a reported £7.3 million ($9.7 million) economic impact on the surrounding area, enough that the town agreed to a new hosting lease this year despite one council member calling the race a "pain in the ass."

Big picture: A full Ironman can take up to 17 hours to finish — and a growing number of people, especially under 30, want in anyway.

Man vs. Horse: The 46-Year-Old Pub Bet That's Still Running

Wales' Man vs. Horse Marathon began in 1980 to settle a pub argument over whether a horse could beat a human on rough terrain. Horses went undefeated until 2004; humans have since won five times, with heat appearing to be the horses' kryptonite. Morning Brew

History

Le Mans: 100+ Years of 24-Hour Punishment

The world's oldest-surviving endurance race gives drivers 24 hours to cover as many laps as possible — often 3,000+ miles — on an 8.5-mile French road course, with no shortage of celebrity sightings along the way. First run in 1923, won by André Lagache and René Léonard. Morning Brew

Health

Which Endurance Supplements Are Worth It?

Science and the endurance community weigh in on the hottest stamina supplements. Morning Brew

✅ Caffeine. A long-trusted pre-race boost; the IOC listed it as a proven performance supplement for elite athletes back in 2018. Cheap and comes in chews, tablets, or just an espresso shot before the start line.

⚠️ Carbmaxxing. A recent study in The Journal of Nutrition suggests athletes may need 90 to 120 grams of carbs per hour during endurance exercise over 70 minutes — up from the ~60 grams previously recommended. Too many, too fast, and the GI consequences are ugly.

⚠️ Sodium bicarbonate. Baking soda wrapped in a hydrogel by Swedish company Maurten to delay lactate buildup without the usual GI distress. Maurten gained attention for fueling Kenyan runner Sebastian Sawe's sub-two-hour marathon this year. Packs of four run ~$70.

⁇ Broccoli shots. Olympic cross-country skiers at Milano Cortina swore by Swedish company Nomio's broccoli juice for lower lactate levels — but the evidence base is thin.

✅ Cherry juice. Tour de France cyclists downed tart cherry juice after every leg last year. The antioxidants appear to genuinely ease soreness.

Extreme

Barely Anyone Can Do These Races

  • Antarctic Ice Ultra — the world's southernmost ultramarathon: up to 100 miles in subzero temps under 24-hour sunlight, for a $22,500 entry fee.
  • Marathon des Sables Legendary — 168 miles through the Sahara Desert in up to 130°F heat, hauling your own gear. In 1994 a competitor got lost for nine days and ended up in Algeria.
  • The Ice Mile — a frigid mile-long swim in water at 41°F or colder. Fewer than 700 people have ever finished one.
  • The Iditarod — a ~1,000-mile Alaskan sled dog race with rules on proper animal gutting, run on two or three hours of sleep a night while mushers watch for wildlife.
  • Sri Chinmoy Self-Transcendence 3,100 Mile Race — run entirely on a single city block in Queens, NY — 5,653 laps in 52 days, roughly two marathons a day.
