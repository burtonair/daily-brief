FAA Issues Rare National Ground Stop, Halting All US Flights for Four Hours

From just before 4pm ET until 8:15pm, the FAA grounded every departing flight in the country — only the second national ground stop since 9/11 — after tech upgrades overwhelmed the aging air traffic control communication system. More than 6,600 Friday flights were canceled, stranding an estimated 1.5 million travelers. Morning Brew

For four hours, all US flights ground to a halt as the FAA halted all planes departing in the United States, citing "extraordinary circumstances and reasons of safety."

At a White House press conference after the stop was lifted, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the incident stemmed from new technology upgrades "overwhelming" the aging air traffic control communication system, and did not appear to be a cyberattack or anything "nefarious." Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said, "I didn't anticipate that we would have an outage like this," and urged Congress to allocate $50 billion to overhaul the system's aging technical underpinnings.

By 9:30pm ET, more than 6,600 Friday flights had been canceled, with another 1,500 scrubbed for Saturday. Travel data firm Cirium estimated 1.5 million travelers were stranded. Air cargo and parcel shipments were also halted, and sports teams — college football, the NHL, and the NBA — scrambled to get to games. Lifting the stop gets passengers moving again, but delays are expected to carry into the weekend as planes, pilots, and crews are out of position.

Duffy said it was "going to be slow" getting air travel back to baseline, but that he didn't expect disruptions during Thanksgiving travel in a couple of weeks.
