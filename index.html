They're Kids. That's What Kids Do.

You asked them three times, and they said they didn't need to go. You just handed them the ice cream they begged for. Everyone was getting along so nicely. And now suddenly they have to go to the bathroom; they've dropped the ice cream all over the floor and themselves; they've hit their brother across the face. Why? Why? Because they are kids. That's what kids do. They drop stuff. They lash out. They have next to no body awareness. This is not a surprise — what's surprising is that you're surprised.

Expect it. Prepare for it. Show them how an adult responds. Easier said than done — in the moment, good intentions aren't enough. You need something that halts the reaction before you do something you'll regret.

The Stoics had a few tactics for this. When Augustus wanted to control his temper, the philosopher Athenodorus told him to recite the alphabet before saying or doing anything in anger. Seneca suggested looking in a mirror to see how anger distorts us beyond recognition.

Next time the ice cream hits the floor, run through the letters, and use those few seconds to remember who you're dealing with. Not an opponent. Not someone trying to ruin your day. A kid. Your kid. With sticky hands and limited awareness, doing exactly what kids do — and remember who they're dealing with in turn: the adult, the parent, the one they're watching to learn what to do when things go wrong.

Which Free AI Tool Should You Actually Use?

ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Grok, and Perplexity all have free plans — but they offer different tools and limits. The Rundown's full guide has you run the same tasks in each one and build a small AI toolkit without paying for any of them:

  1. Open all six services and confirm each account is on its free plan — use personal accounts and keep private work out of the test.
  2. Paste the same fictional Repair Café brief into each assistant and check the event time, staffing, budget, and missing details.
  3. Ask each tool the same question about Shenandoah National Park, then check the leash rule and trail restrictions against the official National Park Service source.
  4. Upload the same small budget CSV wherever the free tier allows it — if uploads aren't available, paste the data and record that limit separately.
  5. Score each result for accuracy, source quality, file handling, and usefulness, then choose a main assistant, a research tool, and a backup.

Pro tip: A free plan can be generous for chat but tight on uploads, image creation, or research — check the limit that applies to the task you actually do.
