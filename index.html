US and Canada Dig In as Trade War Escalates

Talks to avert new US tariffs on Canada collapsed Friday, and both sides are now digging in — the US slapped 50% tariffs on $20 billion of Canadian goods, and Canada has vowed to retaliate dollar for dollar on Sept. 8. Morning Brew

"You're at war when you get attacked," Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said. "We got attacked." President Trump, for his part, wrote on Truth Social that "Canada wants the benefits of being a State, without being one!!!" — the latest volley in a relationship that's grown steadily more contentious.

The new US tariffs primarily hit forestry, alcohol, dairy, and textiles, but the impacted items also include everything from hockey sticks to dog leashes and fake mustaches. Canada hasn't yet released its own retaliation list, but Carney has pledged to match the US levies dollar for dollar, targeting similar industries.

Both countries say the other introduced impossible last-minute conditions after a deal looked close, with sticking points reportedly including tariffs on trucks and steel-based products. It's the latest chapter in a fraught year: after Trump's earlier global tariffs, Canada was one of the few countries to retaliate, going "elbows up" while Canadians boycotted US booze and travel. Looking ahead, the USMCA — the trade deal Trump himself negotiated with Canada and Mexico — is up for review, and this spat suggests that process won't be smooth.

You're Endangering Them for What?

You're driving faster than you should. You pass that car you probably shouldn't pass. You rush through the safety briefing. You don't take the time to double check. You jaywalk instead of taking the longer way through the pedestrian intersection. Why? To save a little bit of time? You're endangering your kids — for a couple extra minutes?

It's crazy. As parents, we spend so much time worrying about our kids. We buckle them into car seats. We make them wear helmets. We tell them to look both ways. We lie awake imagining all the things that could happen to them. And then we take unnecessary risks because we're running late or trying to get to the next thing?

It's extra crazy considering that one of the most common feelings of being a parent is the interminable waiting, the incessant obligation of having to figure out what's next. Yet here you are, trying to rush through something, to make things go faster instead of letting them take their natural course.

And what are you missing in the process? What memory might get made if you weren't so focused on getting there faster? Slow down. Be present. Be safe. Getting this thing over quickly not only endangers your family, but it presents more problems than it solves. Daily Dad

Use Gemini Canvas to Visualize a Google Sheet

Google's new Gemini Canvas might be the fastest way yet to prototype a shareable dashboard straight out of Google Workspace.

  1. Open a spreadsheet — a project tracker or content calendar works well — in Google Sheets.
  2. Launch Gemini in a new tab, click the + button, and select Canvas.
  3. Prompt Gemini: "Make me an interactive dashboard using the data from tabs one and two."
  4. When it finishes, copy the Canvas link and paste it into your browser to view the dashboard outside the chat.
  5. To edit, just keep talking to Gemini in the chat — anyone you've shared the link with will see the updates too.

Pro tip: Gemini can also build fast slide decks in Canvas — ask it to turn the same tabs into slides, then export to Google Slides or PDF.

AI • Health

Lady Gaga's Startup Is Training AI on Living Human Skin

Outer Bio, co-founded by Lady Gaga and fiancé Michael Polansky, emerged from stealth with Yuna — a platform that keeps human skin alive for four weeks and feeds the data into an AI hunting for viable skincare compounds. The Rundown AI

Yuna keeps full-thickness ex vivo skin alive on a 3D-printed scaffold that feeds it and clears waste — about four times longer than skin normally survives outside the body. Data from experiments on that skin trains an AI that proposes compounds likely to affect a given skin process, with each result sharpening the next prediction.

Polansky says the loop has cut discovery time from two leads in ~18 months to a new candidate every six weeks, with six active leads currently in the pipeline. A bioRxiv preprint shows Yuna reproducing known results, including a psoriasis-like state reversed by a JAK inhibitor. It's progress, but still a partial version of skin — with no blood flow or immune cells.
