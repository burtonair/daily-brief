US Unexpectedly Loses 23,000 Jobs, Snapping Growth Streak

The US lost 23,000 jobs last month, according to government data, badly missing analyst expectations of a 100,000-job gain and reviving fears that the economy is losing steam. Morning Brew

The surprise decline was driven by steep losses at restaurants and bars (down 26,000, despite a World Cup boost), big-box retail (down 19,000), finance (down 14,000), and public schools (down 50,000, typical for the summer). Healthcare kept adding jobs (+22,000), and construction and manufacturing gained a combined 27,000 positions, likely thanks to the ongoing data center buildout.

Unemployment actually dropped to its lowest level since 2025 — but not because of a hiring boom. Over 260,000 people left the labor force last month, and the share of Americans working or looking for work is now at its lowest point since the pandemic. The government also revised down job growth for the prior two months by a combined 103,000.

The weak report, paired with yearly wage growth slowing to a five-year low of 3.2%, has traders betting the Fed will skip a rate hike at its September meeting — though next week's inflation data could complicate that calculus if price growth outpaces wages.

AI • Biology

AI Designed 16 Brand-New Viruses That Have Never Existed

Stanford and Arc Institute scientists used an AI program called Evo to design novel viruses not found in nature — a breakthrough that could fight drug-resistant infections but also raises fears about bioweapon misuse.

Evo was trained on over 9 trillion nucleotides and generated roughly 700,000 candidate genomes; researchers tested 285 viable ones and successfully grew 16 real viruses that don't exist in nature, some replicating faster than the natural strain. Engineered viruses are already used to fight drug-resistant bacteria and genetic disease, but the researchers warned the approach shouldn't be applied to risky human pathogens like malaria, given fears a bad actor could repurpose the tech to design a bioweapon.
