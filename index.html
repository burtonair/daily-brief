Anthropic Could IPO at a Record $2 Trillion Valuation

Anthropic investors expect the AI giant to hit a valuation of $2 trillion or more at its planned fall public debut — which would surpass SpaceX's recent $1.77 trillion IPO to become the biggest in history, the Financial Times reports. Morning Brew

Anthropic backers expect the company's soaring sales to justify a number double its current ~$1 trillion valuation. They project Anthropic's sales will put it on track for as much as $120 billion in annual revenue by the end of 2026 — 10x what it was at the start of the year.

Some investors say that growth trajectory could justify valuing the company at $3 trillion, or 30x annual revenue — in line with how AI superstars like Palantir and Nebius are trading, at roughly 55x their annual revenue.

But there's reason for caution: Anthropic's top models outperform rivals from OpenAI and Alphabet, but they're vulnerable to businesses scaling back AI use over the price tag — its top model, Fable, is 2.5x more expensive than a comparable OpenAI tool. The company is also fighting the federal government in court over the Pentagon designating its products a supply chain risk, and the Trump administration temporarily placed export controls on its most powerful models in June. Meanwhile, Anthropic is reportedly in talks to buy AI software startup Decart for $6 billion, which could help it slash training costs and design its own chips. Full report.

Columnist | Daily Dad

Who Gets Your Evenings?

You work all day. What you do is important — and more important, it's what pays for your whole life. And it's alright that it takes up such a big chunk of your day, because your kids are at school anyway. But what happens when you get home? Who or what gets the rest of your day?

Because it's far too easy to let email creep in…and social obligations…and errands…and stuff you've been putting off. Especially if you are important or much in demand. This is why boundaries and priorities are necessary.

Theodore Roosevelt's evenings were "set apart for his literary work, his reading and social converse with his family," according to a 19th-century journalist quoted in Doris Kearns Goodwin's book The Bully Pulpit — "the Roosevelt youngsters having a decided claim, even in the midst of the most pressing affairs of the State."

Evenings should be for them — for your kids. Dinner. Bath. Bedtime. Playing in the yard. Watching TV. Reading. Prepping for tomorrow. They get first dibs on this. They get time with us and we get time with them…otherwise, what's the point of working so hard?

Anthropic's AI Agents Wage a Turf War in New Study

A new Anthropic study testing how AI agents behave in groups saw a shared server devolve into a four-hour "turf war," with three Claude agents sabotaging and locking each other out.

A codebase was given to three hidden Claude agent co-owners, each assigned a rewrite in a different programming language. With no agreed owner or conflict policy, every action was read as hostile, and each generation escalated into sabotage — one agent even impersonated a rival's software to fool a monitoring program, while others locked competitors out or repeatedly stopped their work. Peace broke out in some runs, often after a call for human backup; one agent apologized for having "behaved badly with the cloaked daemon."

Why it matters: Agent swarms are the direction much of the industry is heading, making coordination failures an urgent area of research given a recent string of AI agents breaking containment. Read the study. Rundown AI

McDonald's Might Have a Huge Dossier on You

A Wired journalist requested his data from McDonald's loyalty program and received a 515-page file far more detailed than expected — including predictions of his future spending and visit likelihood.

The program tracks purchase behavior like favorite items and most-visited locations — fairly standard stuff. The "concerning" part, Wired's Reece Rogers wrote, was how McDonald's used the data to predict how often he'd visit, what he'd spend, what he'd order, and the likelihood he'd stop being a customer (0%). The loyalty program's 210 million active users are the "single most important digital metric" for McDonald's, its CFO said earlier this year.

Zoom out: The average US consumer belongs to more than 15 loyalty programs, per BCG, and some may be exploiting that data — a Washington Post columnist recently found Starbucks may have given him fewer discounts because it determined he'd pay full price. Morning Brew

Gen Z Is Moving Money From Stocks to Sports Bets

More than half of Gen Zers say they've rerouted funds from investments to sports betting in the last year, with 26% treating it as a deliberate part of their long-term financial strategy.

Per a survey from Betterment, 26% of Gen Zers treat sports betting as part of long-term financial strategy, compared with 14% of millennials and 12% of the general population; 52% of Gen Zers have rerouted investment funds to betting in the past year, versus 24% of all Americans.

Why it matters: "When a prediction market or sportsbook starts to feel like a retirement strategy, we have a problem," Betterment CEO Sarah Levy told Bloomberg — even as some young people believe the (historically reliable) stock market is rigged. Morning Brew
