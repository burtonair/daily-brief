The Latest Phishing Scam Is Targeting Your Calendar

Incidents of “ICS phishing” — named for the .ics calendar file format — were up ~33,000% between May and September, according to cybersecurity firm Sublime. Morning Brew

Calendar invites sneak malicious links past spam filters without requiring an attachment to be opened, making salespeople and recruiters — anyone who regularly meets strangers — common targets. The links either harvest login credentials, serve malware, or set up a meeting with a scammer posing as someone trustworthy.

One way these invites land on calendars: AI agents accepting invitations on a worker’s behalf. Consumer Affairs suggests checking your settings so you’re not automatically adding every invite that lands in your inbox.

Karpathy’s “Sharp Funnel”: The AI Divide Almost Nobody’s Talking About

OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy revisited his take on how people judge AI, saying it’s widened into a “sharp funnel” where only a tiny fraction sees what frontier AI can actually do. Rundown AI

Karpathy estimates roughly 5,000 people have access to true frontier AI inside labs — capabilities like weeks-long agent swarms or solving open math problems — while nearly 6 billion people (three-quarters of the world) have hardly used AI at all, and another 1–2 billion only use free chatbots for quick answers.

He traces the split to access tiers, problem size, and models that excel where answers are checkable and lucrative, like code and math — calling it “the weirdness of the moment,” with most people underestimating AI while a select few feel “the curve going vertical.”
