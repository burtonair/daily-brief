Let These Ideas Take Firm Hold

Early in his career, the neuroscientist Andrew Huberman worked mostly in isolation. In his lab, he kept a list of the scientists he loved and admired. "I would read that list over and over," he said. "I didn't realize it at the time, but this is what is called introjection."

Introjection is the process of absorbing the qualities and ideas of others into the self. If you regularly listen to so-and-so's podcast, Huberman explains, "the nervous system begins to ask questions like, what would so-and-so do? That's a very real thing." This concept is basically what Daily Dad has been doing for seven years — studying the greatness of people like Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, Fred Rogers, Toni Morrison, Harry Truman, and Angela Merkel. "You must linger among a limited number of master-thinkers, and digest their works," Seneca wrote, "if you would derive ideas which shall win firm hold in your mind."

You study something over and over, the ideas take firm hold. They are absorbed. They become muscle memory, infused into your DNA. We don't just hear about them once — we have to return to their example, their memories, their insights, their victories and their failures, on a daily basis, looking at them from different angles until we have introjected them into our nervous system.

Marcus would later talk about how the philosopher is one with their weapon — like a boxer, more than a swordsman. A boxer just clenches their fist. A fencer has to pick something up. That's what we need to do as parents: create a practice — get the reps — that fuses us with the ideas and qualities of the greats. That inserts those ideas and qualities into our DNA so that we too can be great.

Turn a Spreadsheet Into a Data Story With AI

Claude Data builds a dashboard from a file you upload, with every number linked to the query behind it. Here's how to turn a sales file into an editable dashboard and a shareable data story:

  1. In claude.ai, click Artifacts in the left sidebar, then the Data tile under Make something new. Choose Import a file and attach a CSV, Excel, or JSON file.
  2. In the Reply box, say who the dashboard is for and what they want to know, not only what to chart.
  3. Click the Sources icon at the top right and open a chart's query. Its Result lists the exact values behind the chart, so you can check any figure against your file.
  4. Click the pencil to retitle the dashboard, or ask Claude for other changes, like grouping revenue. You can also open History and restore the previous version.
  5. Ask Claude for a Scrollyteller data story from the same file, and ask what the numbers can't tell you. Check its figures in Sources before you click Share.

Pro tip: Chart labels round off, so a $44,998 quarter can show up as "$45K." When you need the exact number, point at the bar or open Sources to see the figure Claude used.
