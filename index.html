Data Centers Hit the Ballot as OpenAI Plots a Rebrand

This week's U.S. midterms double as a referendum on AI data centers, with roughly 20 communities voting on bans or pauses — while OpenAI reportedly preps a post-election push to rebrand the sites as “AI compute campuses.” The Rundown AI

Democrats are projected to flip the House on Tuesday, which could end a two-year run of easy treatment in Washington for AI firms with close ties to President Trump. Some Democrats, including House hopefuls Will Lawrence in Michigan and Rebecca Cooke in Wisconsin, now want a federal freeze on new data centers. The White House has already gotten tech giants to voluntarily supply their own power, and last week Trump floated having developers pay locals a “dividend” as well.

OpenAI is planning to pitch its campuses as local economic boosts, citing a Pike County, Ohio, project it's helping build that promises thousands of jobs. Policy chief Chris Lehane called the debate “democracy in action,” and the company says it wants state bills that give locals a voice in new projects.

Why it matters: AI has a real image problem with the public, and data centers have become one of the defining fights of Tuesday's elections. That anger won't be fixed by a new name or a few community perks — and a Democratic House could make life a lot harder for AI giants trying to scale their infrastructure bets against local opposition.

Why Are They Like This?

The yelling. The fighting. The mess. The demands. The entitlement. Why are they like this? Your kids are like this because they are kids.

That's why they don't listen. That's why they spill food everywhere. That's why they antagonize. That's why they forget stuff. That's why they constantly ask for things. This is what kids do. This is how their mind works. This is also how the relationship works.

Your job is to understand. Your job is to be patient. Your job is to indulge (not all of it, but much of it). And most importantly, your job is to show them what adulthood looks like. What patience looks like. What it means to handle frustration without losing your temper. What it means to respond to a mistake with understanding instead of lashing out.

Because when they're yelling, how you respond matters. When they're fighting, how you resolve conflict matters. When they're frustrated, how you manage your own frustration matters. They're still learning how to be people. And much of what they'll learn about being adults will come from watching you.

In-N-Out Is Expanding In-N-Out of California

The burger chain is weighing a push into Arkansas, Mississippi, and Oklahoma. Morning Brew

In-N-Out, which got its start outside Los Angeles in 1948, has 445 restaurants in 10 states (289 of them in California). It went farther east than ever last year with a move into Tennessee and plans to open a New Mexico location next year. Because its Tennessee stores get supplies shipped from Texas, owner and president Lynsi Snyder told actor Terry Crews that it could make sense to also open in Arkansas, Mississippi, and Oklahoma, since they sit in the path of that distribution network.

Domestic sales grew about 10% last year, trailing only Shake Shack among U.S. burger chains, per Technomic data cited by the Wall Street Journal.

AI & Space

Google's Space Data Center Sends Home a Haiku

Google's Project Suncatcher satellite answered its first AI prompt from orbit. The Rundown AI

Google's open Gemma 4 model wrote a haiku (“Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core”) that reached the ground as the satellite passed over Svalbard, Norway — part of the company's moonshot goal to put AI data centers in space. CEO Sundar Pichai called it a “hello world” milestone.

Power remains a big constraint: the craft can run just one of its four TPU chips, and only in ~20-minute bursts. Two more powerful satellites launching in 2027 will test the laser links a fleet would need to split one AI job — if the chips survive radiation and heat tests first.
