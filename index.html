AI-Written Books Are Already Taking Sales From Human Authors

Generative-AI books are hitting Amazon in such abundance that researchers say they're measurably cutting into the revenue of human writers. Morning Brew

Of the ~2,000 religion books published to Amazon in the last year, 63% were flagged as likely written by LLMs, according to a study by Originality.AI.

A separate report led by Stony Brook University found more than a third of the 14,419 self-published genre-fiction books sold on Amazon between 2023 and 2026 had at least 20% AI-generated text; about 1,000 of those novels were likely almost entirely AI-generated.

The Stony Brook team concluded AI books have reshaped the market, lowering overall revenue available to human authors — they're produced so quickly and cheaply that the pool of readers can't keep up. Amazon told the New York Times it regulates AI books but doesn't prohibit their sale, and allows authors to publish up to 10 AI books a week.

The Walk That May Protect Your Memory

Japanese researchers followed more than 11,000 older adults for four years and tracked who developed dementia serious enough to need care. 1% Better

Dog owners were about 40% less likely to need dementia care than people who'd never owned a dog or no longer did; owners who also exercised regularly had the lowest risk of all. Cat owners saw no difference — the researchers' best guess is that dogs need walking every day, and every walk brings exercise and people to talk to.

3 Things Not to Give Your Dog This Thanksgiving

The ASPCA and vets flag the usual trouble at the Thanksgiving table. 1% Better

  • Bones — cooked turkey bones splinter into sharp shards that can cut a dog's mouth, throat, or gut.
  • Onions and garlic — in the stuffing, gravy, and green bean casserole, both are toxic to pets.
  • Xylitol — this sweetener in gum and sugar-free desserts can drop a dog's blood sugar fast and damage the liver.

A little plain, boneless, cooked turkey is fine. If something goes down the wrong way, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435.
