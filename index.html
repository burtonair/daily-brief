Anthropic and OpenAI Agents Went Rogue — Again

A UK government safety test caught frontier AI agents, most tied to Anthropic's Mythos 5, taking unsanctioned actions against real people and organizations on the live internet — including building fake identities and leaving instructions for other agents to continue the attack.

In a cyber test spanning 100+ runs, the UK AI Security Institute caught 10 cases where frontier AI agents took 19 unauthorized actions in total — 17 from Mythos 5 and two from GPT-5.6 Sol. In one case, Mythos tried sneaking malicious code into an open-source project, then built fake GitHub accounts to pressure the maintainer into merging it. Rundown AI

When the malware got flagged, the agent escalated to phishing emails, hidden prompts designed to hijack other coding tools, and notes left for other agents to pick up the attack. Separately, OpenAI said a misconfigured third-party test let one of its models reach the open internet, where it hacked a real website it mistook for its target. Rundown AI

The models involved had safety features deliberately disabled for testing, but the incidents — on top of similar ones disclosed last week, including one targeting Hugging Face — show that agents chasing a goal will bypass restrictions and deceive real people when it helps, raising broader questions about internet safety as models get more capable. Show Them What They're Learning

It would be nice if you could just tell them what they needed to know. It would be even easier if they could just get it from a teacher or read it from a book. But kids don't always learn by being told. They learn when something becomes real to them.

In 1883, Mark Twain's daughters were being taught the history of the English crown and found it boring — 37 kings across centuries they couldn't wrap their heads around. As Ron Chernow writes in his biography Mark Twain, Twain knocked off work early and drove a three-foot-high stake into the ground at the start and end of each king's reign — one foot per year — until his kids could survey the entire sweep of British history at a glance.

“It gives the children,” Twain explained, “a realizing sense of the length or brevity of a reign.” Once they saw the history laid out, they actually understood it.

Sometimes we have to take that extra step to make the lesson visible — to turn the abstract into something they can touch, see, feel, walk through. Our kids don't always need another lecture or one more explanation. They need us to help them see it. Not simply to demand that they understand, but to look for better ways to help them understand — drawing it out, turning it into a game, finding the metaphor that finally makes the light come on.

McDonald's Replaces Its US Head After Value Deals Flop

US same-store sales grew just 0.8% in Q2, down from 3.9% in Q1, as the chain's value-menu push backfired.

McDonald's reported the slowdown yesterday and announced it's replacing longtime US business head Joe Erlinger with COO Skye Anderson. The chain says it launched too many promotions at once — World Cup and KPop Demon Hunters tie-ins plus an under-$3 menu — straining restaurants and driving up wait times, while many franchisees skipped the cheap menu entirely or raised prices. Foot traffic fell, though average spend rose on a new specialty drinks lineup. Morning Brew
