The US’ Crude Awakening in Venezuela

President Trump announced a deal giving the US majority control of one-fifth of Venezuela’s oil reserves — about 65+ billion barrels — in an arrangement drawing praise, skepticism, and accusations of corruption in equal measure. Morning Brew

What’s the deal? On Friday, Trump announced the US had struck a deal with Venezuela for majority control of one-fifth of the country’s oil reserves. As part of it, the US will have a direct stake in a private company led by Venezuelan businessman Alejandro Betancourt, which will hold rights to the oil fields for 100 years and become the second-biggest corporate holder of proven oil reserves in the world, behind Saudi Aramco.

Mixed reactions. Trump says the deal will boost the US oil supply and help replenish the Strategic Petroleum Reserve — though that plan faces a Capitol Hill-shaped speed bump since Congress must green-light strategic reserve spending. Some Democrats, including Virginia Sen. Tim Kaine, called the deal “corruption at epic scale,” suggesting it confirms January’s removal of President Nicolás Maduro was motivated by oil rather than drug-trafficking charges. Venezuela’s acting president says the deal will bring tens of billions in investment and thousands of jobs, though hard-liners at home aren’t thrilled with the close US cooperation.

Will it help gas prices? Probably not in the short term — Venezuela’s oil infrastructure will need more than a spit shine to get running again, which could take years.

This Makes All the Difference Daily Dad

You wish your parents could have been different. Maybe a bit more understanding. Maybe a bit more supportive or loving. Now that you’re older, you hear about other people’s grandparents or their extended family, and you wish you had something like that — that source of wisdom, that network, that place you could always go if you needed something.

Well, that wishing isn’t going to get you far. We only get one go-around on this planet, and that’s how your turn ended up. But there is something you can do instead of wish. You can be.

Submit Your iOS App to the App Store with Codex The Rundown AI

Take a mobile app from Xcode to the App Store using ChatGPT Work and App Store Connect — prepare the listing, upload the build, set a price, and submit, without hunting through every screen.

  1. Start with a working app. Tell Codex: “Build this app and send it to App Store Connect.” It will build, archive, and distribute it from Xcode.
  2. Open App Store Connect in Codex’s browser and sign in. Tell Codex to fill the description, upload screenshots, and everything else needed.
  3. Complete the App Review contact, age-rating, and content-rights fields, then select the build. Review each answer before continuing.
  4. Set a price in App Store Connect, then click “Add for Review.” Fix any flags, then click “Submit for Review.”

Pro tip: For a subscription or one-time premium upgrade, ask Codex to add Apple’s StoreKit framework, then set up the product in App Store Connect.

OpenAI Cuts Out Cursor After SpaceX Acquisition

OpenAI announced it will remove its models from coding platform Cursor by Nov. 12, following the company’s acquisition by Elon Musk’s SpaceX — citing Musk’s history of violating contracts. The Rundown AI Morning Brew

OpenAI said it stretched the wind-down as far as its contract allows via cancellation rights that opened once Cursor was sold, calling the decision “incredibly tough.” Its evidence includes the $2M-a-year deal Musk killed after buying Twitter, plus xAI training on OpenAI’s outputs, which Musk called “partly” true.

  • Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown reaffirmed support for Cursor, with many calling out the company’s similar move against Windsurf over its potential OpenAI acquisition.
  • Musk posted “I couldn’t care less,” while Cursor CEO Michael Truell pushed for a fix, putting OpenAI’s slice of Cursor’s overall usage at just 5%.

Why it matters: Cursor has typically been a neutral coding editor carrying all frontier models for developers to choose from. Now it finds itself in the middle of the Altman-Musk feud — though with xAI’s Grok surge and Cursor’s own model progress, competition might play a bigger role than the politics.
