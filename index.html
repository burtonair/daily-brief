Anthropic Pledges 5% of Shares to a New Public Benefit Fund

The frontier AI developer is putting real equity behind the industry's talk of sharing AI's windfall with the public — though who actually gets paid is still unannounced. The Rundown AI 1% Better

The structure: Anthropic introduced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned limited liability company that would get 5% of the company's equity if shareholders sign off — about $48B at its May valuation. The board may add a further 2% for every 50% rise in Anthropic's value above an unannounced baseline, "at the board's discretion." Anthropic chose an LLC over a 501(c)(3) specifically so the fund can make direct distributions to specific populations, not just donate to nonprofits — and none of it can go to lobbying or political giving in the US or abroad.

The target: The company flagged AI's toll on workers as one use case, building on the $200M it committed in June to study job disruption and test policy responses. Counting the new fund, the co-founders' 80% giving pledge, and stock Anthropic matches when staff donate, the company now counts roughly 20% of its shares as earmarked for public good.

Why it matters: Universal basic income and other public-benefit structures have been floated throughout the AI boom, and this is the clearest sign yet of a frontier lab putting real equity behind that idea. But Anthropic already has a governance body for this — the Long-Term Benefit Trust, whose Class T stock elects a board majority. The new fund, by contrast, is so far a large number without managers or recipients. Until money actually reaches anyone, the public may have trouble separating this from a PR play.

Turn a Content Spreadsheet Into a Working App

Using Airtable Omni, turn a content plan into an app that shows what needs review, who owns it, and what's overdue — then add a checkbox that moves a complete draft into review.

  1. Create an empty practice base in Airtable and open a new Omni chat. Turn off internet access for the exercise.
  2. Upload your content plan and campaign list. Ask Omni to propose the tables and campaign links before building them, keeping missing owners and dates blank.
  3. Verify the links, then build a review page with counts, a content list, and owner filters.
  4. Ask Omni to create an automation that moves a complete draft into review when you tick a checkbox. Inspect the setup and test one record before turning it on.
  5. Test an item with a missing owner and one that's approved — neither should move — then confirm a valid draft updates correctly.

Pro tip: A missing owner should stay blank in the data. Writing "Unassigned" into that field could let an incomplete draft pass the check.

"No on Prop 40" Spending Tops $1 Million a Day

The opposition campaign against California's billionaire wealth-tax ballot measure is outspending backers ahead of next month's vote, per new Guardian reporting. Morning Brew

The SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West introduced Prop 40 last year to recoup $100 billion for healthcare after federal funding cuts under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act. The measure would impose a one-time 5% tax on billionaires' wealth, with 90% of proceeds earmarked for healthcare over five years.

Opposition group Building a Better California, led by Google co-founder Sergey Brin, has run ads featuring the presidents of the California Medical Association and Planned Parenthood Northern California urging a "no" vote. The race is tightening: a late-September UC Berkeley poll found yes votes down to 45% and no votes up to 42%, from 48%–41% in early August. If it passes, it would be the world's first billionaire tax.

Anthropic's Own Data Shows Junior Employees Losing Ground

A new Anthropic workforce study finds junior technical hires shrinking as a share of staff while AI agents absorb more of the workload. The Rundown AI

  • Junior roles fell from 27% to 9% of Anthropic's technical hires; about a third of ML managers say AI has them hiring less.
  • Employees' agents logged 6x the hours of the humans running them by August, and engineers now push 5x the code changes they did six months ago.
  • Claude agents sit idle roughly 80% of the time in back-and-forth sessions, waiting on a person to respond — humans, not models, are now the bottleneck.
  • Year-plus staffers switch departments 50% more often than in 2025; Anthropic is building AI-risk alerts per role to help move people to less-exposed jobs.

The hiring data echoes warnings CEO Dario Amodei has made for over a year — but the bigger reveal may be that agents are now producing more than staff can review.
