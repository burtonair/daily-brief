Columnist | Daily Dad

The Parts of Us That Are Still Growing

It hasn't been that long — just a few years since they were born. But it's undeniable: they've changed so much. They're bigger, smarter, taller. They look different, talk different. That's what kids do. They're always growing.

But while they were changing, weren't we changing too? We're becoming different parents — more experienced in some ways, more humbled in others. We're learning what matters, what doesn't, what we could control, and what we absolutely couldn't. We're moving through our own phases of life right along with them.

This is what the Stoics meant when they said life was change.

It's also why certain books stick with you for decades. You pick up the same page five years later and find something completely different in it. A sentence you barely noticed before suddenly feels like it was written just for you. The words haven't changed — but you have.

Protect Your Files With a Claude Code Mod

Build a small Claude Code add-on that asks for approval before its editing tools touch a chosen file — then test it by declining one edit and approving the next. Full guide here.

  1. Open a new practice folder in Claude Code and add the guide's two sample files, so you can test without touching real work.
  2. Paste the guide's prompt and ask Claude to build the mod — it should show the proposed change and ask before editing your chosen settings file.
  3. Review the code Claude creates and run the checks in the guide. Fix any errors, then load the mod in a new session.
  4. Ask Claude to edit an ordinary file, then the settings file. The settings change should trigger your new approval question.
  5. Decline the change and confirm the file stays untouched. Repeat, approve it, and check the result.

Pro tip: Keep Claude's permission checks on. This mod only checks two editing tools — other tools can still change the file.

Supreme Court Weighs Climate Liability in Boulder vs. Big Oil

Justices heard arguments on whether Boulder, CO's suit against Exxon and Suncor over climate damages can proceed — a ruling that could open or close the door on billions of dollars in similar suits nationwide. Morning Brew

  • Boulder frames its suit as consumer protection — modeled on opioid and tobacco cases — not emissions regulation, arguing taxpayers shouldn't foot the cleanup bill.
  • Exxon and Suncor counter that greenhouse gas emissions are federally regulated, not left to states; the Trump administration agrees.
  • Justice Samuel Alito recused himself over fossil-fuel investments, raising the chance of a 4-4 split. A ruling is expected by June.
  • Other cases on the new term's docket: third-country deportations, Ten Commandments displays in schools, and state AR-15 bans.
