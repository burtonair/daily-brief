Columnist | Daily Dad

If You Want a Crowded Table

It can sometimes be helpful to sit back and really think about what parental success looks like. Is it raising kids who succeed in school? Or in their careers? These things are nice, but in the big scheme of things, that's probably not what we should be aiming for.

Of course, it's having healthy kids — that's obvious. But when you flash way forward into the future, imagining your and their lives many years from now, what do you hope to see? Almost certainly it's something close to the idea captured in The Highwomen's hit, Crowded Table — all of you together, getting along, spending time enjoying each other. At Thanksgiving. On birthdays. At some summer house you all rent as a family.

This doesn't just happen. You don't get a table full of smiling faces for every holiday just because you're related. A crowded table is a lagging indicator of the job you've done as a parent. And it starts early — or it starts now, if you realize there's something you need to repair.

You'll need to make the right decisions now so that someday your kids will want to fly from their homes to yours when they're older and have families of their own — or be willing to invite you to theirs. Set the table today to have the one you'll want tomorrow.

Check Black Friday Prices With Gemini Tasks

Today's AI Training guide walks through using Tasks in Gemini to check the price of one thing you want to buy this Black Friday week — comparing six stores in a table, checking whether the discount is real, and re-checking daily until a date you choose.

  1. Go to Gemini on the web or app, sign in with your personal Google Account, and select Task — pick Hydrogen if you can choose a model
  2. Paste the guide's price prompt with your own product and stores, and set limits: web search only, no sign-in or purchase
  3. Open the lowest link, then the lowest one where the store itself is the seller; check the model, that it's new, who sells it, price with shipping, and the return window
  4. In the same chat, ask whether the price is a real discount — the list price, the lowest price with evidence before this month, and where each number comes from
  5. If you can wait, ask Gemini to check the same stores daily until December 1 and only flag a drop below your price

Pro tip: Put your limits in the first message — the exact model, which stores, who must be the seller, and what Gemini must not touch. Then open the store's page before you pay.

Nate's Notebook

What Five Cents Builds Now With AI

Rundown AI educator Nate Grahek built a TV ad-muter for live sports in under 30 minutes, 95% accurate on the first try, for 5 cents a game — then kept snowballing the idea.

Tired of loud ads during live sports, Nate had his Mac take a picture of the TV every second and classify it as "game" or "ad," muting automatically when an ad appeared. He expected the project to cost $10–20 a game; it cost 5 cents. From there he added agents that scrape the funniest posts across networks for the family to read during breaks, and even swapped the TV announcers for the radio broadcast.

His takeaway: "My experience is my biggest asset, and it's also my biggest blind spot — I keep assuming complexity and costs that aren't there." Before scoping version one of anything, ask AI what you're assuming is too hard or expensive to try. Watch the breakdown →
