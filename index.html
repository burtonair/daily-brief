Maybe It's Priceless?

They have everything. Money. Power. Fame. The billionaires. The celebrities. The world-movers. They have it all, except somehow for all that, they can't buy one thing: being home when their kids get home from school. The ability to drop everything and go pick them up when they need something. Being there for bedtime. Showing up for games, school plays, recitals, parent-teacher conferences.

In short, those billionaires, those celebrities, those world-movers can't do the very thing that you not only can do—but sometimes lament having to do. Don't you see how lucky you are? Don't you see how rich you are?

You get to be around. You know your kids' teachers. Your relationship with your baby is not intermediated through nannies or long-distance telephone calls. You are the chauffeur, and that means you get so much time with them. You get to be there when they score a goal or play their first solo.

If a billionaire can't afford something, maybe that's because it's priceless. Daily Dad

Use AI Skills to Make Better Decisions, Quickly

Give Claude Code a routing table of decision-making skills so you can turn problems into solutions on demand, instead of starting from a blank prompt every time.

  1. Open Claude Code in Terminal and install makerskills with /plugin marketplace add coreyhaines31/makerskills, followed by /plugin install makerskills@makerskills.

AI • Medical Tech

UK Surgeons Perform First Brain Surgery With a Live AI Assistant

A University College London system watched a live surgical camera feed in real time, flagging hidden arteries and optic nerves during the removal of a patient's pituitary tumor. The Rundown AI

Patient Rhys Hibbert had no symptoms until a seizure in late 2024 revealed an 11mm growth narrowing his field of vision. Surgeons reached the tumor through his nose while the AI—trained on hundreds of labeled videos of past procedures—marked safe zones and no-go areas on its own monitor. Lead surgeon Hani Marcus said the system had effectively seen "more operations than most surgeons see or do in a lifetime." Hibbert's vision was restored within days, and the team is now moving toward larger trials.

Six Flags Roller Coaster Linked to Brain Injuries, Two Deaths

The X2 coaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain has been closed since July after two women fell into comas with similar brain hemorrhages days apart, per a CNN investigation. Morning Brew

One of the women, Pamela Guillen, regained consciousness after two weeks; Naomi Greer-Wilkinson has not yet awakened. Court and medical records reviewed by CNN link the ride to two prior deaths (2010 and 2022) and more than a dozen serious injuries over its 20-plus year history. Six Flags declined to comment on the recent cases; a lawsuit from one of the earlier deaths was set for trial next month but settled this week on undisclosed terms.
