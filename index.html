Banks Scramble as AI Agents Go Rogue Inside Customer Accounts

Top U.S. banks — JPMorgan, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, and Citi — are restricting third-party AI agents after catching them logging into customer accounts and taking actions nobody approved, with executives calling the situation "horrifying." The Rundown AI Morning Brew

The problem: Banks caught agents logging in with customers' own credentials against the rules — sometimes calling support lines or taking unauthorized actions the user never requested, Axios reported. Most cases trace back to user-authorized agents rather than hacks, but one bank executive admitted they're struggling to tell a human from an AI assistant.

The response: Morgan Stanley is blocking third-party agents outright; JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Citi are restricting them. Most banks are moving toward a middle ground of read-only access rather than letting agents take action on their own.

Why it matters: With real money on the line, blanket blocks can't last forever as agentic AI adoption grows — but they buy banks time to build frameworks that let vetted agents act while catching rogue ones, which Axios says are also increasing.

Why Can't You Be There?

They'd love to be there, but they can't. They can't make it. They have a meeting. They have work. They have an opportunity they just can't turn down. Well…they could turn it down, they just won't.

Obviously, there are some parents who can't be as hands-on as they'd like because they're working just to survive. That's difficult and unfair. But what about the parents who are incredibly successful — the ones who've made it to the top, who make great money, who own their own businesses — yet still aren't there for school pickup, still work long hours, still seem to always be away?

It's sad and tragic in a different way. We're not wage slaves, but we are, to borrow an expression from Seneca, "slaves to our ambition." We say we're "doing this for our family," but we've long since passed that point. If anything, this is taking us away from our family.

At some point, we have to stop pretending we don't have a choice. We do. The meeting can be missed. The call can wait. The opportunity can be passed on. The question isn't whether you can be there — it's whether you're willing to make being there the priority.
