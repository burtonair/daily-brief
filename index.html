Columnist | Daily Dad

Show Them What It Means to Be a Giver

It's a world with too many problems. It's a world with too much suffering. It's a world with too much selfishness. We can't fix all of this as people, nor as parents. But we can try to help a little.

We're trying to raise kids who are "the opposite of spoiled" — not just self-sufficient, but with energy and resources left over to help others. You want to raise kids who are decent and kind, who are not entitled, who are part of the solutions, not the problems, of the world and of their generation.

So what's the secret? There isn't one. Except, as we've said over and over again, modeling the traits you'd like to see in them. If you want generous kids, you have to show them what a generous parent is. If you want your kid to be a helper, you have to show them what being a helper looks like.

There is no better time than the holiday season to do this. While it's easy to slip into the temptation of materialism, you can use this time to focus on people who are less fortunate — by volunteering, by donating, by thinking about what others need rather than what you want. You can—you must—be what you want to teach your kids. It will make them better, and it will improve you in the process.

How To | The Rundown AI

Give Jobs to Nod, the Personal Agent From n8n

Today's AI Training guide walks through setting up Nod, the free personal agent from n8n Labs, and giving it three jobs: compare free plans, get approval before submitting a form, and run a scheduled weekday news check.

  1. Go to nod.n8n.io, download Nod for Mac, drag it to Applications and open it
  2. Select Get started, then Continue without email; choose your provider and sign in with ChatGPT or paste in an API key
  3. Ask for a comparison you can check — e.g. the free plans of Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams — in one short table with a link to each source
  4. Give Nod a practice form and end the prompt with "then ask me before you submit it"
  5. Ask for a recurring job, such as a 9 a.m. weekday check of the OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind news pages

Pro tip: Write three things into every request: what done looks like, which sites/apps Nod may use, and what it must ask you first. Run each schedule once and read its cost before you leave it on.

Rowan's Corner

The Simplest Tip to Improve AI Output

Before any important ask, add one line to your prompt: "Ask me five questions before you answer."

The biggest reason people get generic answers is that AI guesses at your situation instead of asking about it — this line makes it tell you what it's missing. To make it fast, don't type your answers; hit the microphone button and ramble for a minute so it has plenty of context. The whole flow: write your ask in one or two sentences, add the line, then answer by voice. Watch the clip →

How a Town With No Sun Looks Forward to Winter

Today the sun sets in Tromsø, Norway, and won't rise again until January — yet a study of nearly 9,000 residents found less winter depression than the darkness would suggest.

American researcher Kari Leibowitz moved there to find out why and kept running into the same thing: people look forward to it — candles, bonfires, skiing, northern lights.
