Is Caviar Losing Its Status as an Elite Delicacy?

Chinese caviar now accounts for 43% of the global market, up from 14% in 2012 — flooding wholesale prices and letting everyone from McDonald's to scrappy DTC startups get in on what was once an untouchable luxury. Morning Brew

Caviar wasn't always hoity-toity — 1800s American bars served it free, like peanuts. Its luxury status later spread from Europe and Russia to the US just as overfishing, especially in the Caspian Sea, decimated sturgeon stocks. By 2006, bans from the US and the United Nations had effectively halted the import and export of wild-caught caviar. Morning Brew

That pushed suppliers from fishing to farming — a slow, finicky business since sturgeon can take decades to mature. In the 1990s, China invested heavily in sturgeon aquaculture, and by 2024 it was the largest global supplier, accounting for 43% of the market, per a Financial Times analysis of International Trade Centre data. Why it matters: Cheaper wholesale caviar has let nontraditional vendors pile in — McDonald's topped McNuggets with it for Valentine's Day, and a wave of new DTC brands has popped up selling tins for under $200, versus $500+ from traditional suppliers. Read more Morning Brew

Have You Eaten These Foods? A Global Traveler Test

Beyond Ibérico ham and Italian white truffles lies a world of prized cuisine that most tourists never try.

  • Durian — the pungent Southeast Asian fruit whose international market grew 400% between 2022 and 2023, driven by demand in China.
  • Kibbeh Nayyeh — Lebanon's version of steak tartare, made with minced raw lamb, bulgur, and Lebanese seven-spice.
  • Escamoles — cooked ant larvae, sometimes called the caviar of the desert, running $50 a pound.
  • Hákarl — fermented Greenland shark buried and dried for months; Anthony Bourdain vowed never to eat it again after one try.
  • Cuy — guinea pig, a special-occasion meal in Peru dating back to Incan times.
  • Giant African land snails — hand-sized snails in soups and kebabs, routinely seized by US Customs.
  • Noddy bird — a seabird caught in Nauru for centuries using bird calls and netted poles; one visitor says it tastes like chicken, but better.
