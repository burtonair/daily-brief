Warren Buffett Steps Down as Berkshire Chair, Hands Reins to Son

After more than six decades at the helm, 96-year-old Warren Buffett is stepping down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, with his son Howard taking over the role effective immediately. Morning Brew

Buffett announced the change in a letter to shareholders: "Father Time always wins," he wrote. "He has, however, been generous with me." He had already handed the CEO role to Greg Abel at the end of last year, and will now become chairman emeritus while remaining on the board. Details. Morning Brew

During Buffett's tenure, Berkshire grew from a struggling textile mill into a $1 trillion conglomerate, with shares up more than 5,500,000% — versus a 39,000% gain for the S&P 500 over the same stretch. Howard, 71, has sat on Berkshire's board for 33 years and has served on the boards of Coca-Cola and ConAgra Foods, among others. Morning Brew

Why it matters: CEO Greg Abel remains in charge of operations; Howard's job, per Buffett, is simply deciding "whether the board…may need to change the CEO" — a culture-and-values keeper rather than an operator. Morning Brew
