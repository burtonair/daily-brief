Green Cards Frozen at Eight of Tech's Biggest Employers

The Labor Department has stopped accepting — and halted processing of — green-card sponsorship applications from Microsoft, Adobe, Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL, and Capgemini, while VP JD Vance separately accused Microsoft of replacing American workers with "foreign indentured servants." 1% Better Morning Brew

Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said yesterday the department would stop accepting new PERM labor-certification applications — a required step in many employer-sponsored green-card cases — from the eight firms, attributing the Microsoft and Adobe suspensions to active federal investigations. Vice President Vance separately announced Microsoft's suspension from the H-1B-to-green-card pipeline entirely. 1% Better

Vance cited 6,000 layoffs, 6,300 H-1B visas, and nearly 3,000 green cards issued by Microsoft in the past year as evidence of worker replacement. Microsoft disputes this, saying roughly 80% of its ~6,000 H-1B applications last fiscal year simply extended or changed the status of existing employees rather than hiring new ones. 1% Better

The legal basis is a labor-regulation provision that lets the department suspend PERM processing during a fraud investigation — without a conviction — for an initial 180 days, extendable. It's not a finding of fraud, but it halts a step many top employers rely on to keep sponsored workers, some of whom are nearing the six-year cap on H-1B stays. The EB-1 and EB-2 national-interest-waiver routes offer a bypass. The next thing to watch: whether the suspension notices and investigative basis become public. Go deeper. 1% Better

Columnist | Daily Dad

Don't Wish Away a Minute of Their Life

It's understandable. It's reasonable. You're tired. They're tired. It's been a long day or a long drive. You're ready for the school play to be finished up. You're ready for bedtime. You're ready for them to be out of diapers. You're excited for them to start college.

In the book Travels with Epicurus, Daniel Klein tells a lovely story: on an overcrowded train to Philadelphia, a young woman moaned to her mother, "God I wish we were there already!" Her white-haired mother replied, "Darling, never wish away a minute of your life."

That's what we — supposedly the wiser adults — are constantly doing ourselves. And worse, we are wishing away our kids' lives. The most beautiful and cute and fleeting part, too.

Don't wish it away, no matter how frustrating this phase or this bedtime or this age is. Before you know it, they'll have grown. They won't need you as much anymore. You won't see them every day. And you'll wish for it all back.

Clean Up a Messy Excel Report with Claude

Use Claude's Excel add-in to clean up a messy sales export, fix a wrong total, and build a report — then test whether a new order correctly updates the chart. Full guide here.

  1. Install the official Claude add-in and open a copy of your workbook.
  2. Tell Claude what each row means and which orders to count. Ask it to find duplicate rows, missing details, and formula mistakes before changing anything.
  3. Approve the fixes on a separate sheet. Keep the original data and ask for a record of each change, including any rows left out.
  4. Build a monthly report with formulas and a chart. Check the order count, refunds, and sales total against the source rows.
  5. Add a test order and confirm the report and chart update, then remove it and confirm the original totals return before saving.

Pro tip: A spreadsheet can show a wrong total without showing an error — ask Claude to explain which rows it counted, then spot-check a few yourself.

The Most (and Least) Lucrative College Degrees, Revealed

New data crunched by the HEA Group shows Duke math majors out-earning nearly everyone else four years after graduation — but a looming federal rule could cut loans to the lowest-earning programs. Morning Brew

Duke's math undergrads earn a median $297,029 four years out — the highest of any US program. Computer science programs at Carnegie Mellon, UCLA, MIT, Pomona, and several Ivies start at $217,000, and California nursing majors land around $150,000.

Among the liberal arts, English majors median out at $48,920, trailing history majors by $50,000+. A proposed rule could cut federal loans to undergraduate programs that don't beat the $34,808 median high-school-grad income; Juilliard says its 2026 projections will clear that bar.
