Basketball Is Reaching New Heights

Interest in basketball is surging worldwide and leagues are expanding on every front — except, notably, the players’ shorts, which keep getting smaller. Morning Brew

Around the world: Basketball is the world’s second-favorite sport, trailing only soccer, according to a Nielsen Sports survey of 18 countries conducted on behalf of FIBA. Interest in the game grew from 68% to 74% between 2019 and 2023, and last season’s NBA opening night featured 135 international players from 43 countries. The league is also expected to launch NBA Europe in 2028, per The Guardian.

Beyond the NBA: Growth isn’t limited to the league itself. The WNBA just posted its best regular-season viewership since 2000 and plans to reach 18 teams by 2030 — six more than it had in 2024. College basketball is expanding March Madness from 68 to 76 teams next year.

In another league: Upstart leagues are cashing in, too. Ice Cube’s three-on-three BIG3 league is valued around $290 million, and Unrivaled, a women’s three-on-three league, just raised funds at a $650 million valuation — almost double what it was a year ago.

Knicks IPO Comes Amid a Blowout Year for MSG Stock

Shares of the reigning NBA champs are expected to list on the NYSE this month as Madison Square Garden Sports spins off its Knicks business from the Rangers. Morning Brew

MSG’s holding company market cap is nearing $10 billion, up more than 80% from the same time last year — with the Knicks accounting for most of that value. In private-market terms, the Knicks are worth an estimated $11.75 billion per JPMorgan, versus an estimated $4.15 billion for the Rangers, per Sportico.

The move isn’t without controversy: incoming Knickerbockers Corp. board member Isiah Thomas was previously accused of sexual harassment, and owner James Dolan has faced scrutiny and protests over MSG’s use of facial recognition technology.

How a Nobel Economist Helped Close the WNBA Pay Gap

Claudia Goldin, a Nobel Prize-winning economist who says she doesn’t even watch basketball, advised the WNBA players union to a 400% pay bump. Morning Brew

Goldin crunched the numbers behind players’ claim that pay didn’t reflect the league’s rising popularity, powered by stars like Caitlin Clark and Angel Reese. She found the WNBA drew roughly 30% as many monetizable broadcast viewers per player as the NBA, and argued the $127k average 2024 WNBA salary versus the NBA’s $10 million average was an “embarrassing” gap.

The resulting deal pushed average salaries to an estimated $583,000, expected to top $1 million by 2032. Goldin rejected the idea the gap reflects unprofitability, and other economists noted the NBA spends 50% of revenue on player salaries versus a much smaller WNBA share. Viewership grew 17% last season, though some question whether that pace holds.

From Dress Codes to Thom Browne: Basketball’s Fashion Evolution

Tunnel ’fits have come a long way from the NBA’s strict business-casual mandate, and both leagues now treat style as part of the draw. Morning Brew

Allen Iverson’s look is rumored to have prompted the NBA’s 2005 dress code, which critics said targeted Black players by banning durags, chains, and baggy clothes. The rules loosened under commissioner Adam Silver — during the 2018 playoffs, LeBron James and his Cavaliers teammates donned custom Thom Browne suits, and players were allowed mismatched sneakers starting that season.

The WNBA has moved past its own jeans ban: in 2024, No. 1 pick Caitlin Clark became the first basketball player dressed by Prada for draft night, and the league named Coach its official handbag partner.
