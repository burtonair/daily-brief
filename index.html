You're Not Enough People — and You're Not Supposed to Be

You're not just a parent. You're a chauffeur. You're a cook. You're a teacher, a guidance counselor, a nurse. Some of these roles might come naturally to you, but the rest might make you feel like an imposter — and when you drop the ball on dinner, or show up to the wrong field for practice, maybe you think you've failed.

But maybe that's not actually the problem. During an argument with his wife, Kurt Vonnegut realized they were expecting something impossible from each other. "When a couple has an argument," he said, "they may think it's about money or power or sex, or how to raise the kids, or whatever. What they're really saying to each other, though, without realizing it, is this: 'You are not enough people!'"

That's certainly true of parenting. For most of human history, parents didn't do this alone — there were grandparents and siblings and neighbors around to wear some of these hats, people you could ask, "Is this normal?" Most of us don't have that kind of village anymore, and yet we expect one or two exhausted adults to do the work that used to be spread across a whole group of people.

We are not meant to parent alone. We are not enough people to possibly know everything, handle everything, and figure all of this out ourselves — and you were never supposed to be enough people all by yourself.
