Bleisure Travel Goes Mainstream: 83% of Business Travelers Now Mix Work With Play

What started as a 2009 marketing coinage has become standard practice: the vast majority of American business travelers now tack personal time onto work trips, and Gen Z is leading the charge toward $1.7 trillion in projected bleisure spending by 2032.

They say never mix business with pleasure — but nothing is stopping you from combining business with leisure. Known as “bleisure travel”, the practice of tacking extra days onto a work trip exploded after the pandemic, thanks to more remote and hybrid jobs that can be done from anywhere.

Eighty-three percent of American business travelers at least occasionally turn a work trip into leisure, per a survey from the travel app Skyscanner. Forty-two percent add one or two days onto business trips, and nearly 40% of corporate buyers say trips are simply growing longer, according to the Global Business Travel Association. Gen Z loves it most: nearly two-thirds of the youngest workers who travel for business are taking part in bleisure, per a 2025 Harris Poll, and nine in 10 Zoomers see the trips as a chance to elevate their lifestyle — upgrading to first class or a nicer hotel — according to Hotels.com.

The best bleisure destinations, per Booking.com research, are New Orleans, Las Vegas, Orlando, Boston, and Washington, DC. Forbes pegs the bleisure market at $685 billion in 2024, projected to hit $1.7 trillion by 2032. Morning Brew

AI Is Getting All Up in Business Travel — From Expense Fraud Detection to Booking

Travel tech firms Navan and Amadeus say AI is removing friction from expense reports and catching fraud — per AppZen, more than 3.5 million phony AI-generated receipts surfaced in just six months, up 30% year over year. Business • Executives

Flying Private Is Now a Standard Top Exec Perk

The majority of Fortune 500 CEOs are now flying private on the company dime, per Equilar, with corporate jet spending up 19% between 2021 and 2024. Palantir spent $17.2M flying CEO Alex Karp in 2025 — more than double the year before — and Meta’s Zuckerberg travel bill hit $2.4M, up from $968K in 2023. Morning Brew

Orlando Ranks No. 1 Meeting Destination in North America — 11 Years Running

Nearly 6 million people visited Orlando for meetings or conventions last year, per Visit Orlando, up 3.1% — outpacing the city’s overall tourism growth. Disney World and Universal are a major draw for bleisure-minded attendees. Morning Brew

Debate Club • Workplace

Should Companies Be Allowed to Make Employees Share Hotel Rooms?

It’s not technically illegal — but HR and legal experts strongly disagree on whether it should be.

The case against: Most HR and legal experts strongly advise against required roommates. It obliterates downtime, destroys personal boundaries between colleagues, and the lodging savings aren’t worth the risk of a harassment allegation.

The case for: Splitting a room saves money and time — one alarm instead of two. Proponents argue the awkwardness of a coworker’s toothbrush-borrowing or late-night FaceTiming is a small price for a company that can then afford extra pizza toppings at the next office party. Morning Brew
