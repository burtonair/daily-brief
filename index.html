Airline Perks Are Taking Off (But They Might Cost You)

Free wi-fi, roomier seats, and beefed-up loyalty programs are reshaping the flying experience — but airlines are also finding new ways to make you pay for the comfort they used to give away.

Wi-fi — once a pricey add-on — is now mostly free and widely available, with JetBlue the first US airline to offer it in 2017 and the rest of the industry following. The catch: most airlines require signing up for their loyalty programs to get it, and those programs have become major moneymakers. A Business Insider analysis of SEC filings found loyalty programs netted more than $6 billion each for American and Delta in 2024, and more than $2 billion each for United and Southwest. Morning Brew

Seating is getting a makeover too: United announced it will roll out rows that guarantee no middle seat, plus economy rows that fold into couches next year, a feature Air New Zealand already offers. Emirates, meanwhile, is fitting planes with fold-out headrests. Morning Brew

At the same time, Delta and United have been experimenting with stripping free perks from a lower fare tier — no refundable ticket, fewer free checked bags — so they can charge less for those seats while courting wealthier travelers willing to pay extra to upgrade. Morning Brew

Your Robotaxi Might Narc on You

Waymo called the police on two teen riders after an employee monitoring the live camera feed thought they were shooting a real gun — it was actually Orbeez gel pellets. The company shut the car off remotely and blamed “mechanical issues” to keep the teens seated until police arrived; they were released to their parents without arrest. Waymo staff can also check in over the speakers if riders fall asleep, and remote agents have called young-looking passengers to confirm they're old enough to ride solo. Morning Brew

Air Taxis May Become the Quickest Way to the Airport

Electric vertical takeoff and landing aircraft could get travelers from Manhattan to JFK in 10 minutes instead of an hour-plus, per demo flights from Joby Aviation. The DOT approved eight pilot programs across 26 states in March; prices could start near Uber Black rates before falling to as little as $25 by 2030. Morning Brew

Car-Free on Mackinac Island

Michigan’s Mackinac Island has banned internal combustion engines since 1898 — walking, biking, and horse-drawn carriage are the only ways around. About 300 workhorses arrive each summer to ferry roughly 1.2 million tourists and haul everything from garbage to mail. Morning Brew

Why Japan Has the World’s Best Trains

The Shinkansen bullet train network averages a 1.6-minute delay — and once issued a formal apology for leaving 25 seconds early.

Japan’s Shinkansen can replace a six-hour drive with a two-hour ride at a top speed of 200 mph. In Japan, 28% of passenger miles are traveled by train, compared with 10% in France and just 0.25% in the US. Morning Brew

Experts trace the network’s reliability to the privatization of the country’s railroads in the 1980s, when an inefficient national railroad was split into six leaner companies that cut unprofitable routes. Morning Brew

Those companies don’t just run trains — they own real estate and businesses near stations, from amusement parks to retirement homes. A third of East Japan Railway Company’s revenue last year came from hotels, real estate, and retail, funding further service upgrades in a virtuous cycle: a quality passenger experience keeps trains full, which funds the next round of improvements. Weeks-long staff retreats also produce low turnover and high professionalism. Morning Brew
