If Anything, You Should Be Slowing Things Down

We are always rushing our kids, rushing through things — as if it's not clear where all this is heading. We are rushing them as if rushing through this doesn't mean having to come up with the next activity, doesn't bump up into them asking us for screen time, as if we're not rushing through their childhood, through our time with them. If anything, we should be slowing down.

Right now, we have them. We are at the grocery store on a Saturday. That's wonderful! Why are we trying to finish that quickly? Right now, we have them in front of their school, watching them chat with their friends. Why are we telling them to wrap it up? Right now, we have them, whether they are a 2-year-old, a 12-year-old, or a 20-year-old. Why are we trying to get this over with?

This is life. Not only is there not a finish line — the finish line is the thing we are desperately trying to avoid. At some point, they won't need us to drive them. To wait for them. They won't need us any longer. They will be adults.

This is what we're going to miss. Yet here we are, rushing through it, rushing them. Stop! Or at least, slow down. They're growing up before your eyes. Make sure you see it.
