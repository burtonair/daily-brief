Isn't This What We Want to Pass Down?

It's one of the most unbelievable stories in all of history. A child is born into slavery and becomes one of the most influential philosophers not just in their own lifetime, but of all time.

Epictetus's name, in Greek, translates to "acquired one." In Rome, the law of the empire — Lex Aelia Sentia — was that no slave could be free before their 30th birthday. From those thirty years as the property of a violent and depraved master, in a Roman Empire known for treating its slaves cruelly, Epictetus would learn about the ultimate power human beings hold. This lesson would go on to guide emperors and exiles, war heroes and prisoners of war, and men and women all over the world for thousands of years. It is a lesson we parents must teach our kids — or the world will do it for us.

We are powerless over so much. We can be tossed around by the oceans, we can be struck down by disease, we can be literally put in chains. But each of us has power over the greatest empire: our minds. Our attitudes, our wants, our desires, our opinions about what has happened to us — Epictetus said, "even Zeus cannot deprive you of that fortune."

And in a world full of misinformation and misdirection, isn't this the inheritance we want to pass down? To show our children how to master their minds and hold onto their inner freedom, no matter the situation? But first, we must know how to do so ourselves.

An a16z Partner Built a Fake AI Sorority Girl — and TikTok Believed Her

a16z partner Olivia Moore revealed that Janie, a redheaded "19-year-old" whose University of Alabama sorority recruitment videos pulled ~1M views on TikTok in a week, was an AI character created and run for around $100. The Rundown AI

Moore created Janie from a single ChatGPT image, then spent about 30 minutes a day prompting MiniMax and Grok Imagine to animate her daily posts. Janie racked up followers and mainstream press coverage even after viewers caught the AI tells just days into the experiment; TikTok flagged 8 of 20 videos with an AI label, though it didn't hurt performance.

Moore didn't disclose the AI use during the run, but after revealing the experiment said the "overwhelming majority of feedback was positive." Why it matters: her post-mortem frames the real question as disclosure and effort, not whether a creator is "real" — with AI video nearing the point of being indistinguishable from reality.

American Airlines Bringing Back Seatback Screens by 2028

American will begin installing screens in the seatbacks of every plane in its fleet in 2028, reversing a decision to rip them out about a decade ago. Morning Brew

American had bet flyers would prefer personal devices, while saving on fuel and repair costs. But it has since fallen behind rivals Delta and United. Along with the screens, planes will get Bluetooth connectivity, USB-C ports, more first-class seats and legroom, and Starlink wifi on 500 planes starting next year — though most upgrades won't be complete until the early 2030s.

Screens mean dollars: they let airlines show personalized ads to a captive audience, something United started doing in 2024.
