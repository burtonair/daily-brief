AI Is Rewriting How College Students Pick Majors

Nearly a quarter of students say they've already switched fields over AI job-market fears, and computer science — the fastest-growing major for 15 years — just saw enrollment dip for the first time. Morning Brew

As students return to campus, AI isn't just doing their homework — it's reshaping what they choose to study in the first place. In a survey of US college students by Superhuman, 69% said they feared AI would make it harder to find a job, and 22% said they'd changed their major or concentration because of it. A separate survey from the Lumina Foundation and Gallup found 13% of bachelor's students and 19% of associate's students had switched fields over AI, with 47% saying they'd given the idea "a fair amount" of thought.

They're not wrong to worry: researchers at the St. Louis Fed found that AI has created "an additional headwind" for recent grads chasing jobs. The clearest casualty is computer science: after 15 years as the fastest-growing major, CS enrollment dipped 8.1% last year, per the Washington Post.

But the fix isn't obvious. Only four schools offered a dedicated AI major in 2020; now 79 do, and 100 more offer a minor or concentration, per Northeastern researchers. OpenAI's VP of education tells Axios that employers will prize transferable skills — critical thinking, leadership, collaboration — over any single degree. Other schools are betting on new territory entirely: Arizona State's influencer major teaches content creation as a viable career track.

Columnist | Daily Dad

Teach Them How to Choose

It makes sense that you and your partner would make most of the decisions for your kids. You know more. Your kids basically know nothing — about life, about what the weather is going to be tomorrow, about how the world works. You, on the other hand, have been 7 years old as well as 17 years old before.

The problem is that constantly deciding for them deprives your kids of a very important skill: the ability to make decisions. Is it any wonder so many teenagers are utterly overwhelmed when it comes to choosing where to go to college, or what to major in? For most of them, it's the first real decision they've ever made.

That's why, as a dad, you have to actively work to not choose everything for them. Ask them whether they want to go to the park or play catch in the yard. What should we cook for dinner? Shorts or pants today? Let them pick out something to wear.

Teach them how to choose. Empower them. Make sure they know how to decide — and to be okay with it, even if they decide wrong. It doesn't matter that you know more. Let them learn. Life is decisions. Prepare them for it.

A Neurosurgery Resident Just Solved a 22-Year-Old Math Problem With ChatGPT

Beijing neurosurgery resident Shanmu Jin proved Crouzeix's Conjecture, a matrix problem unsolved since 2004, after a 16-hour autonomous session with GPT-5.6 Sol.

Jin reached the problem through brain-ultrasound research, teaching himself advanced math after training in geology and medicine. His setup denied the model internet access and pitted swarms of subagents against each other — a technique borrowed from one of OpenAI's own math prompts.

Formal peer review is pending, but Cornell's Alex Townsend, Anne Greenbaum, and the conjecture's original author have all verified the proof. Why it matters: when a proof can come from anyone with a ChatGPT subscription, verifying it becomes the scarce, in-demand skill. The Rundown AI
