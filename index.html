Business • Entrepreneurship

You Don't Need to Change the World to Get Rich

Forget the VC-backed app pitch — a growing wave of entrepreneurs is getting rich off "sweaty startups": unglamorous, physically demanding local businesses like power washing, storage, and carwashes. Morning Brew

There are two types of business pitches: raise VC funding for an AI-powered plumber booking tool, or borrow some cash from your mom to start a company that actually fixes people's toilets. Many enterprising folks are lured by the latter path — rejecting innovative business ideas to found unglamorous companies like carwashes or childcares, known as sweaty startups.

The term was coined by entrepreneur and content creator Nick Huber, who started a storage business while studying at Cornell in 2011, grew it to 25 college towns, and eventually sold it for $1.7 million. He now makes content advising aspiring entrepreneurs on starting low-risk businesses that don't need to be explained to anyone — detailed in his book The Sweaty Startup: How to Get Rich Doing Boring Things. His pitch: old-school local businesses succeed more reliably than trendy startups because competitors are weak and demand is stable. His recommended businesses include mold removal, firewood delivery, and portable toilet rental.

The numbers back him up: the share of US income earned by the 1% ($550k+) from midsize regional businesses like flooring and plumbing has grown to 34.9% in 2022, up from 30.3% in 2014, per University of Chicago economists' analysis. And Gen Z is rolling up its sleeves: business-start applications from the age group grew 66% in the year leading up to June, more than any other age group, per the Bank of America Institute.
