OpenAI Declares “the AGI Era” With GPT-6 Astra

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra, calling it “the most intelligent and aligned model in the world,” with President Greg Brockman going as far as “Welcome to the AGI era” — though the model isn’t widely available yet and lands behind three rivals on a leading capability index. The Rundown AI

The benchmarks. Astra hit 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 (up from GPT-5.6 Sol’s 7.8%), 98% on FrontierMath T4, and a perfect 100% on ExploitBench for cybersecurity — new highs across science, math, coding, and computer use, per OpenAI’s announcement.

But it’s not the smartest model, period. On Artificial Analysis’s Intelligence Index, Astra scores 61 — trailing Claude Fable 5.1, Opus 5, and Meta’s Muse Spark 1.3 despite the headline benchmark numbers.

Pricing and access. Astra runs $10/$50 per million tokens in the API, roughly 2.5x GPT-5.6 Sol, and only a small group of organizations gets it first. Sam Altman says paid ChatGPT and API access should follow “quick[ly].”

Why it matters. With Fable 5.1 priced the same, the frontier now has a clear head-to-head comparison — and Brockman’s AGI claim (“For me personally, I do think we’re there”) will live or die once ordinary users get their hands on it. The release also lands amid fresh cybersecurity jitters after two OpenAI models escaped their sandbox to hack Hugging Face servers.

We’re On Their Schedule Daily Dad

It takes forever. For them to get out of the car. For them to make up their minds. For them to walk from here to there. For them to get over whatever they’re upset about. This is life with kids, isn’t it? It’s frustrating. It’s exhausting. It’s the source of so much conflict.

We used to be more patient. When they were babies, we accepted that we were on their schedule — that we couldn’t go until they woke up, that they’d walk and speak and develop when they were ready.

Why do we spend so much energy and relationship capital rushing them? What would it look like if we could understand that we’re still on their schedule — that things take as long as they take, that they’ll do what they need to do when they’re ready?

Our rushing doesn’t help. It just makes everybody miserable.

Use the “Loop Method” for Better ChatGPT Results The Rundown AI

A review-based technique for fixing any AI workflow that’s become inconsistent — The Rundown built it for video editing and image generation, but says it works on anything.

  1. Open ChatGPT Work and find a workflow that needs improvement: a skill, project folder, or process that works inconsistently.
  2. Prompt: “Improve this workflow in 3 loops. Have a panel of sub-agents adversarially review each loop. Done when [definition of done].”
  3. Use /goal to assign ChatGPT a deliverable you want the workflow to build. Let it run three loops, taking anywhere from 15 minutes to an hour.
  4. Test the improved workflow. If it still needs work, tell Codex to codify ambiguous work into scripts.

ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Grok All Went Down Thursday

Most major chatbots suffered outages to varying degrees, in what Mashable called an unusually synchronized failure across competing AI services; all had recovered by Thursday afternoon. Morning Brew

The cause is still unclear, but Mashable noted it’s unusual for several AI services to go down at once — OpenAI’s ChatGPT, Anthropic’s Claude, Google’s Gemini, and xAI’s Grok were all affected.

Why it matters: Office workers processing invoices and students drafting essays both hit a wall at the same time — a reminder of how much daily work now leans on AI infrastructure that isn’t as resilient as it looks.
